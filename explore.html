2 知识库

本节课目标

完成本节课堂训练及课后练习后，学生应能：

  1. 核验文献及其版本身份，记录来源、校验值与页码口径，区分已读原件与待核材料。
  2. 区分原文观察、跨文献归纳与研究假设，并使每项归纳都能追溯到具体证据。
  3. 比较不同研究的实验条件，判断表面冲突属于条件差异还是机制分歧。
  4. 将文献分歧转化为带竞争解释、可区分预测和事前判据的研究问题。
  5. 通过抽查原文和检查合并过程，评价分工阅读与知识整理的质量。

课堂以三篇论文训练上述方法；扩展到十四篇论文、处理反例与校准并行复筛，在课后独立完成。

课前完成准备，课堂 60 分钟，课后完成复习、独立实践与迁移评价，另可选做深入专题。

课前准备 · 课堂教学 · 课后复习与扩展

本节的分析材料是一组关于带噪标签下鲁棒损失的文献。其中两篇论文都比较鲁棒损失，方法排名却相反。若只作摘要式归纳，容易得出各有优劣的结论。研究性阅读应当继续追问：两篇论文的数据是否相同，学习率日程是否相同，报告的是最后一轮还是最好一轮的结果。

回答这些问题，需要一个能把每项判断追溯到原文的知识库。关系图帮助查找材料，证据表则说明材料能支持哪些判断。

课堂教学

文献分歧记录的分析，5 分钟

先查看 preflight.md，说明本次使用自己的原件还是预览材料，再进入同一条分歧。

示例库的 conflicts.md 记录了 SCE 与 GCE 的排序反转，并保留两篇论文各自的设置和页码。它没有把全部差异归于单一原因，因为学习率和其他训练参数也同时改变。

这张表提供了一个实验入口：在同一实现中固定其他设置，只改变学习率日程，观察排序是否随之变化。与摘要中的一句性能优越相比，这样的问题能够直接决定下一项工作。

论文页、概念页与问题页的分层，10 分钟

论文页记录原作者在具体设置下观察到的结果。概念页综合多篇论文，说明哪些认识已经相互印证，哪些依赖特定条件。问题页提出研究者自己的解释，并写出能区分它与另一种解释的实验。

三层之间用链接连接。原文观察、跨文献归纳和研究者的推测各有明确身份，更新时便知道应修改哪一层。概念页即使写得流畅，也须能逐条追溯到论文页。

练习一：核验三份原件，10 分钟

从 papers.md 选编号 2，5，11，核对对应 PDF 的标题，作者与版本。
保存到 vault/sources/，记录来源 URL，取得时间，SHA-256 和 PDF 页数。
标明后面页码按 PDF 顺序页还是印刷页计。
若缺全文，记录可用入口与当前原因，先用 preview/ 中对应笔记练习，不冒充已读全文。

同一篇论文的不同版本可以并存，但须分别记录身份。版本不同时，表号和数字也可能不同。

练习二：分工阅读与证据表，20 分钟

三篇论文分给独立阅读助手，每个只读指定文件，写一份论文页。
每项结果记录设置，指标，数值与原文位置。原作者解释单独写，自己的推测另列。
主会话抽查一条核心结果与一条方法条件，再合并为一个鲁棒损失概念页。
把不能直接比较的条件列入 conflicts.md，说明需要怎样的同条件实验。

阅读助手只提交文件位置和未解决项。主会话遇到关键矛盾时回读相关原文。概念页最后由一个会话负责合并，避免多个写作者相互覆盖同一结论。

练习三：由分歧提出可检验问题，10 分钟

只读当前知识库，提出一个研究问题。
写两种竞争解释，分别预测哪项可测结果会不同。
给出最小实验、主要指标和事前判据，注明来自哪些证据行。
另请一个新会话先找最近的一项先例，分别比较问题与干预，再核对指标和结论，其余查新留到 2.3。
存为 questions.md 和 novelty.md。

评价这一问题的标准是：若实验结果符合解释 A 的预测，会更新哪条认识；若符合解释 B 的预测，又会更新哪条。两种结果都能更新知识库，问题才有继续研究的价值。

课堂检查与本节小结，5 分钟

常见错误与处理方法

把设置不同的结果称为矛盾。 先对齐比较单位。暂时无法对齐的，记录为条件差异，再设计同条件实验。

把笔记当作结论的二手来源。 概念页保留通向原文的链接，关键判断回读原文抽查。

完全依据引用数确定阅读名单。 先按研究问题覆盖不同方法与负结果，再借助引用关系补漏，新近工作另行检索。

查新后只说明没有完全相同的工作。 给出与最近先例的逐项对照，说明新实验能多回答哪个问题。

本节小结

浏览器用于获取外部原件，文献接口提供可复查的标识，Obsidian 使研究者与 Agent 共读同一批文件，Sub-agent 使独立阅读可以扩展。知识库中可追溯的基本单位是一条带实验设置的证据，下一课据此设计实验。

课后复习与扩展

概念复习、正反例分析及能力达成评价中的独立实践与迁移任务为本节必做内容；其余深入专题按研究需要选做。

概念复习，15 分钟

关闭概念页，画出课堂上的一条证据链：从原件到论文页，再到概念页与问题页。凭记忆写出每一层的职责，再打开文件核对。选一条结果回到原文，检查版本、页码和设置，补完课堂标为待核的项目，写入 review-after-class.md。

正反例辨析，20 分钟

笔记甲写道：两篇论文排名相反，因此两者的机制互相否定。笔记乙分别列出学习率日程、噪声和选择轮次，并提出固定其他设置、只改变日程的实验。判断两份笔记各能支持哪一层结论。再为笔记乙加入一篇反例文献，沿链接列出哪些页面需要重新判断，哪些原文记录应当保留。

写完更新清单后，再查看参考思路

甲把条件差异直接当作机制冲突。乙先记录观察，再提出可区分的解释。反例加入后，原件和历史观察保留不变，当前概念、竞争解释与实验问题按依赖关系复查；只修改概念页标题不算完成更新。

扩展学习

先把课堂上的三篇论文补成完整证据链，再选择一个专题。扩展到十四篇论文时沿用同一模板，最终在自己的研究方向上提出三个各有竞争解释和判别指标的问题。

能力达成评价

评价分三个层次。课堂训练层面，检查三篇原件的身份记录是否完整，论文页是否区分观察、作者解释与推测，研究问题是否给出两种解释的不同预测。课后独立完成层面，学生把知识库扩展到十四篇论文，加入一篇反例，并说明哪些判断因此改变、依据是哪几条证据。迁移层面，学生在自己的研究领域选取一组结论不一致的文献，独立判断分歧来源，提出三个可检验问题，并为每个问题说明两种可能结果分别会怎样更新现有认识。评价重点为证据追溯、比较条件及研究问题的推理依据。

归因判断须区分已排除与尚未排除的解释。学生应列明当前证据能否支持归因，并提出完成归因所需的同条件判别对照；证据不足时，明确判定为尚不可确定。

2 Research knowledge bases

Learning outcomes

After completing this session and its review and extension work, students should be able to:

  1. Verify the identity and version of literature sources, and record the location in the source of every result they use.
  2. Distinguish original observations, cross-study generalizations and research hypotheses, and maintain the links between these layers.
  3. Compare the experimental conditions of conflicting studies and determine whether a disagreement reflects competing mechanisms or differences in conditions.
  4. Convert a disagreement into a testable research question with competing explanations, distinguishing predictions and a comparison with the nearest precedent.
  5. Evaluate the quality of a knowledge base or of parallel reading by its traceability to sources and the conditions it omits.

The outcomes are assessed at three levels. In class, students build an evidence chain for three papers. For independent work after class, they extend the knowledge base to fourteen papers, add a counterexample and explain how it changes their judgments. For transfer assessment, they analyze conflicting conclusions in their own research field and propose three questions, each with competing explanations, discriminating predictions and an account of how the possible results would update the knowledge base.

Preparation before class, a 60-minute class session, then required review, independent work and transfer assessment, with optional further study.

Preparation · Class session · Review and extension

A research knowledge base is useful when it allows each conclusion to be traced to the observations that support it and the conditions under which they were obtained. Summaries that omit conditions tend to reduce real disagreements to statements that each method has strengths and weaknesses. Research requires further questions: whether the data were the same, whether the learning-rate schedule was the same, and whether results were taken from the last epoch or the best one.

The case material for this session consists of two papers that compare robust losses under noisy labels and report reversed rankings of the methods. The session builds a knowledge base that traces such questions back to the originals. The link graph helps locate related material; the evidence table shows which conclusions that material can support.

Class session

Documenting a disagreement, 5 minutes

Confirm from preflight.md whether the class work uses the student’s own originals or the preview, then examine the disagreement.

The sample knowledge base’s conflicts.md records the reversed ranking of SCE and GCE and keeps each paper’s settings and page numbers. It does not attribute the difference to a single cause, because the learning rate and other training parameters also changed.

The table provides a starting point for an experiment: in one implementation, fix the other settings, change only the schedule and observe whether the ranking changes. Unlike a claim of superior performance in an abstract, this question directly determines the next piece of work.

Three layers of evidence, 10 minutes

A paper page records what the original authors observed under specific settings. A concept page synthesizes several papers and identifies conclusions supported across studies and those that depend on particular conditions. A question page states the student’s own explanation and describes an experiment that can distinguish it from an alternative.

The three layers are connected by links. Original observations, cross-study generalizations and the student’s own speculation are recorded separately, so that an update can be applied to the correct layer. A concept page that reads fluently must still trace back, line by line, to paper pages.

Exercise 1: verifying the three originals, 10 minutes

From papers.md, choose items 2, 5 and 11 and check the title, authors and version of each corresponding PDF.
Save them to vault/sources/, recording the source URL, time obtained, SHA-256 and PDF page count.
State whether later page numbers follow the PDF page order or the printed page numbers.
If a full text is missing, record the available entry point and the current reason, and practice first with the corresponding note in preview/, without pretending the full text has been read.

Different versions of the same paper can coexist, each with its own recorded identity. When versions differ, table numbers and values may differ as well.

Exercise 2: dividing the reading and building the evidence table, 20 minutes

Give the three papers to independent reading helpers. Each reads only its assigned file and writes one paper page.
For each result, record the setting, metric, value and location in the original. Write the original authors' explanation separately, and list my own speculation separately again.
The main session spot-checks one core result and one method condition, then merges them into a single concept page on robust losses.
List conditions that cannot be compared directly in conflicts.md, stating what experiment under matched conditions is needed.

Helpers return only file locations and unresolved items. When the main session encounters a key contradiction, it rereads the relevant original. A single session is responsible for merging the concept page, so that several writers do not overwrite the same conclusion.

Exercise 3: from disagreement to a testable question, 10 minutes

Read only the current knowledge base and propose one research question.
Write two competing explanations, and for each predict which measurable result would differ.
Give the minimal experiment, the main metric and the criteria set in advance, noting which evidence rows they come from.
Also ask a new session to find the single nearest precedent and compare it item by item on question, intervention, metric and conclusion; leave the rest of the novelty search for 2.3.
Save as questions.md and novelty.md.

The test of the question at the end of class is this: if the experiment shows A’s prediction, which conclusion would be updated, and if it shows B’s prediction, what would change? A question is worth pursuing if either result would change the knowledge base.

Common errors and summary, 5 minutes

Common errors and corrections

Calling results from different settings a contradiction. Align the unit of comparison first. Differences that cannot yet be aligned are recorded as differences in conditions, and an experiment under matched conditions is then designed.

Treating notes as a secondary source of conclusions. Concept pages keep the chain to the originals, and key judgments are spot-checked by rereading the original.

Letting citation counts determine the whole reading list. First cover methods and negative results by question, then fill gaps through citation relations, and run a separate search for recent work.

Concluding a novelty search with the statement that no identical work exists. Give an item-by-item comparison with the nearest precedent and state which additional question the new experiment answers.

Method summary

The browser provides access to external originals, literature APIs leave identifiers that can be rechecked, Obsidian lets the student and the agent read the same files, and Sub-agents allow independent reading to scale. The traceable unit is now a piece of evidence together with its setting; Session 3 turns such evidence into an experiment.

Review and extension

Conceptual review, analysis of contrasting examples, and the independent and transfer tasks specified in Assessment are required for this session. Other extension topics are optional, except for the required readings identified below.

Unaided review, 15 minutes

With the concept page closed, draw one evidence chain from class: from the original to the paper page, then to the concept page and the question page. From memory, write what each step is responsible for, then open the files and check. Take one result back to the original, check the version, page and setting, complete the items marked as outstanding in class, and record everything in review-after-class.md.

Analysis of contrasting examples, 20 minutes

Note A states that the two papers rank the methods in opposite order and concludes that the mechanisms the papers propose contradict each other. Note B lists the schedule, the noise and the selected epoch separately, and proposes an experiment that fixes the other settings and changes only the schedule. Judge which level of conclusion each note can support. Then add a counterexample paper to B and, following the links, list which pages must be reassessed and which records of the originals must be kept.

Record your update list before reading the commentary

A treats a difference in conditions directly as a conflict between mechanisms. B records observations first and then proposes explanations that can be distinguished. When the counterexample is added, the originals and earlier observations are kept, and the current synthesis, the competing explanations and the experimental questions are rechecked along their dependencies; renaming one concept page does not complete the update.

Assessment

Assessment has three levels. In class, students establish the identity of the three originals, distinguish observations, authors’ interpretations and conjectures in their paper notes, and specify the different predictions of two explanations for a research question. For independent work, students extend the knowledge base to fourteen papers, add a counterexample, and explain which judgments change and which evidence supports the changes. For transfer assessment, students select a set of papers with conflicting conclusions in their own field, independently analyze the sources of disagreement, and formulate three testable questions. For each question, they explain how two possible results would update the current understanding. Assessment focuses on evidence traceability, comparability of conditions and the reasoning behind each research question.

Attribution must distinguish explanations that have been ruled out from those that remain possible. Students must state whether the evidence supports an attribution and specify the controlled comparison needed to establish it. Where the evidence is insufficient, an explicit judgment that the cause remains undetermined is appropriate.
