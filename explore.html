2 Agent 探索

一小时后，你有一个关于带噪标签学习的知识库：十四篇论文的全文，每篇一页笔记，每条结论带原文页码；几页概念页把论文连在一起，一张表列出论文之间的分歧；最后是三个能用第 1 课那个小实验检验的问题，每个都写好了预测。

课堂核心 60 分钟。课后深入 2.1 至 2.3 共约一个半小时，按需选读。

下载第 2 课练习材料

练习材料的核心是一份论文清单 papers.md：十二篇在 arXiv 上，两篇在出版社网站上，订阅内容要图书馆的机构登录才能下载。另有一个空的知识库骨架和一个做好的示例库。课题接着第 1 课：训练标签有错时，网络先学规律后记错误，什么办法能挡住后一段。

开课先装工具，1 分钟发出去

本课暗线：让 Agent 能出门找资料，回来存成你也能读的知识库。 第 1 课里 Agent 用的都是你放进文件夹的东西。这一课添四样：一个替它登录出版社的浏览器，一个你和它共用的笔记库，一组查论文元数据的接口，以及把几十篇论文分给多个助手并行读的 Sub-agent。

在 Claude 里打开 lesson-2 文件夹，先发：

先读 ../lesson-1/AGENTS.md，把里面的工具用法抄进本文件夹的 AGENTS.md。
这个项目还要从网上拿论文全文，并把读书笔记存成 Obsidian 知识库。检查并补上：
1. Ego Lite 浏览器和它给 Agent 用的 ego-browser Skill，按 https://lite.ego.app/ 的说明装；
2. Obsidian，把本文件夹下的 vault/ 设成一个库；
3. 确认你能调用 arXiv 和 OpenAlex 的公开接口，各查一篇论文的题目给我看；
4. 派一个 Sub-agent 读 papers.md 的前三行，只回报它读到了几篇，证明并行助手能用。
装之前列出打算装什么、装在哪，等我同意。需要我在图形界面里点的步骤，停下来告诉我点哪里。
装完在 AGENTS.md 里补几行：论文元数据先查接口，订阅全文用 Ego 浏览器，笔记写在 vault/ 的哪里。

Ego 装好后打开它，用你学校图书馆的账号登录一次出版社或者图书馆门户。之后 Agent 用这个浏览器时，沿用你已登录的状态，不需要你把密码告诉它。用 Windows 的话，可以用 Claude in Chrome 扩展代替 Ego，道理相同。

先看成品，5 分钟

打开练习材料里 preview/ 下的示例库，用 Obsidian 打开。左边是论文页，一篇一页；中间是概念页，比如记忆化，早停，鲁棒损失，每页开头一段综述，下面列出哪些论文说了什么，链接回论文页；conflicts.md 是一张表，列出两篇论文在各自什么设定下，给出了相反的结果或相反的做法。打开关系图，论文和概念连成一张网，孤立的点是还没和别的页面连起来的论文。

questions.md 里是三个问题。每个问题写成一个题目加一段摘要，摘要里给出预测的结果和数字，最后一行注明它从哪几页笔记里来。

讲，10 分钟

先定名单，再拿全文。 让 Agent 上网搜索论文，它会给你一些看起来很像真的题目和作者。名单要从文献接口来：arXiv 和 OpenAlex 返回的是数据库里真实存在的记录，每条带 arXiv 编号或 DOI，可以核对。名单定了再下载全文，每一篇都核对页数和首页题目。

浏览器是 Agent 的手。 订阅论文要机构登录，有协议页、跳转和下载按钮。普通的命令行下载拿不到，或者拿到一个登录页。让 Agent 驱动一个你已登录的浏览器，它就能像你一样点进去下载。遇到真人验证，它停下来叫你，你点完它接着干。

一个对话读不了几十篇。 对话越长，早先读的论文越模糊。把论文分给 Sub-agent，每个只读三四篇，按同一个模板写笔记到文件，主对话只看笔记，不看原文。

知识库要能被你读。 笔记存成 Obsidian 里的 Markdown 文件，你在 Obsidian 里读，Agent 在文件夹里读写，两边看到的是同一份。每条结论写清原文页码，你随时能翻回去核对。

练习 1：拿到十四篇全文，15 分钟

读 papers.md。arXiv 上的论文用 arXiv 接口下载 PDF；只在出版社网站上的，用 Ego 浏览器按 DOI 打开，下载全文。
全部存到 vault/sources/，文件名用第一作者姓加年份。
每个 PDF 核对两件事：页数大于零，首页或第二页上能找到论文题目里的词。对不上的，说明拿到的是什么。
结果写进 vault/sources/manifest.csv：编号，题目，来源，文件名，页数，题目核对是否通过。
遇到真人验证时停下来叫我；协议和同意页面可以点同意。

两篇出版社论文是这一段的重点：看 Ego 窗口里 Agent 怎样自己点开图书馆登录，找到下载按钮。

练习 2：并行读，写成知识库，20 分钟

把 vault/sources/ 里的十四篇论文分成四组，每组交给一个 Sub-agent。
每个 Sub-agent 只读自己那几篇，按 vault/templates/paper.md 的模板，每篇写一页笔记到 vault/papers/：
研究的问题，实验设定，主要结果带数字，每条结论注明原文页码，和我们课题的关系一句话。
Sub-agent 只返回写了哪些文件，不把笔记内容贴回来。
全部写完后，你读所有笔记，建三到五个概念页到 vault/concepts/，每页一段综述，下面列出相关论文说了什么，用 [[双链]] 链回论文页。
再写 vault/conflicts.md：哪两篇论文在什么设定下结论相反，各自的数字和页码。

写完后在 Obsidian 里抽两页笔记，按页码翻回原文，看数字对不对。

练习 3：提三个能检验的问题，10 分钟

开一个新对话，只给它知识库，不给它我们之前的对话：

只读 vault/ 下的概念页和 conflicts.md，以及 ../lesson-1/ 里的实验代码和结果，不读别的。
提三个研究问题，每个都能用 ../lesson-1/code/run_experiment.py 改几行、在笔记本上一小时内检验。
每个问题写成一个论文题目加一段摘要，摘要里写明预测的结果和数字，以及什么结果会推翻它。
最后一行注明它来自哪几页笔记。存到 vault/questions.md。

然后在 Codex 里查新：

读 vault/questions.md 和 vault/papers/。逐个判断这三个问题是否已经被知识库里的某篇论文回答了；
再用 OpenAlex 按关键词查一次，列出最接近的三篇论文和它们与这个问题的差别。不要改问题本身，结论写到 vault/novelty.md。

被查出已有答案的问题，回到 Claude 换一个。留下来的三个问题，第 3 课会挑一个真的去跑。

最容易出错的地方

下载到的不是那篇论文。 出版社的通用下载链接有时给的是版权协议表格、投稿检查表，甚至参考文献里的另一篇论文；慢的网站会给一个零页的残缺文件。文件名对，内容不对。每个 PDF 都核对页数和首页题目，对不上就换来源：预印本，作者主页，机构库。

把同意页当成验证码，或者反过来。 登录途中弹出的隐私、cookie 和授权同意页，Agent 可以自己点同意；真人验证和图片验证码才需要你。在任务卡里写清这条分工，它就不会每一页都停下来问你，也不会去硬闯验证码。

一个对话读几十篇。 读到第二十篇，前十篇已经记不清，概念页里会出现张冠李戴的数字。论文交给 Sub-agent 分组读，笔记落成文件，主对话只读笔记。

笔记没有页码。 没有页码的结论没法核对，过一个月连你自己也不敢用。模板里把页码设成必填项。

问题从对话里来，不从文献里来。 你在同一个对话里聊过自己的想法，它提出的问题就会悄悄向你的想法靠拢，看起来都很好，其实是你说过的话。提问题用一个只读知识库的新对话，每个问题注明来自哪几页笔记。

名单收得太窄。 一开始就让它只要最相关的五篇，后来发现漏了一条线，补起来比删多余的难得多。名单宁宽勿窄，读完再删。

在 AI 科学家里。 AI 科学家把读过的论文存成一片文献森林：每篇论文拆成设定、结果和主张，按时间截断，用早先的论文去预测后来论文的结果，猜得准的认识才算学会了。本课的知识库是它的一个小样，问题来自文献而不是来自对话，这条规矩也是从那里来的。

换成你自己的方向

挑一个你熟悉的小方向，让 Agent 先用 OpenAlex 查近三年引用最多的二十篇，你删掉不相干的，再走一遍练习 1 到 3。用你自己的判断去检验它提出的三个问题：哪个你愿意让学生去做，为什么。

2 Exploring with Agents

In an hour you will have a knowledge base on learning with noisy labels: fourteen full papers, a page of notes for each, and a source page number for every claim. A few concept pages connect the papers, and a table lists their disagreements. You will also have three questions you can test with the small experiment from Session 1, each with a prediction written down.

Core class: 60 minutes. Self-study sections 2.1 to 2.3 take about an hour and a half in total; read what you need.

Download the Session 2 materials

The exercise centres on a paper list, papers.md: twelve papers on arXiv and two on publisher sites, where downloading the subscription content needs your library’s institutional login. There is also an empty knowledge base structure and a finished example. The topic follows Session 1: when training labels are wrong, the network learns patterns first, then memorizes errors. What can stop that second phase?

Install tools at the start: send the card in 1 minute

This session’s hidden thread: let the agent find material and bring it back as a knowledge base you can read too. In Session 1 the agent used only what you put in the folder. This session adds four things: a browser that signs in to publishers for it, a shared notes folder, APIs for paper metadata, and Sub-agents that split dozens of papers among assistants to read in parallel.

Open the lesson-2 folder in Claude and send:

First read ../lesson-1/AGENTS.md and copy its tool instructions into this folder's AGENTS.md.
This project also needs to get full papers online and save reading notes as an Obsidian knowledge base. Check and set up:
1. The Ego Lite browser and its ego-browser Skill for agents, following https://lite.ego.app/;
2. Obsidian, with vault/ under this folder opened as a vault;
3. Access to the public arXiv and OpenAlex APIs: look up one paper with each and show me its title;
4. A Sub-agent: have one read the first three lines of papers.md and report only how many papers it found, to show the parallel assistant works.
Before installing, list what you plan to install and where, and wait for my OK. If I need to click something in a graphical interface, stop and tell me where to click.
Then add a few lines to AGENTS.md: use APIs for paper metadata first, Ego for subscription full texts, and where in vault/ to write notes.

Once Ego is installed, open it and sign in to a publisher or library portal with your university library account. When the agent uses this browser later, it reuses your login; you do not need to tell it your password. On Windows you can use the Claude in Chrome extension instead of Ego. The idea is the same.

See the finished product, 5 minutes

Open the example knowledge base under preview/ in Obsidian. On the left are paper pages, one per paper. In the middle are concept pages, such as memorization, early stopping and robust losses. Each opens with a short overview, then lists what each paper says and links back to its page. conflicts.md is a table of pairs of papers that reach opposite results or recommend opposite approaches under their respective settings. Open the graph view: papers and concepts form a network. Isolated points are papers that have yet to be connected to other pages.

questions.md holds three questions. Each is a title and an abstract with predicted results and numbers. Its last line says which notes it came from.

Talk, 10 minutes

Settle the list before getting the full texts. Ask an agent to search the web for papers and it may give you titles and authors that look real. Get the list from literature APIs: arXiv and OpenAlex return records that actually exist in a database, each with an arXiv ID or DOI you can check. Settle the list, then download the full texts. Check every paper’s page count and first-page title.

The browser is the agent’s hand. Subscription papers need an institutional login, with agreement pages, redirects and download buttons. A command-line download fails or returns a login page. Give the agent a browser you have signed in to, and it can click through and download as you would. At human verification, it stops and calls you. Once you click through, it continues.

One conversation cannot read dozens of papers. As it grows longer, the papers read early on become hazy. Split them among Sub-agents. Each reads three or four and writes notes to files using the same template. The main conversation reads only the notes, not the originals.

You need to be able to read the knowledge base. Notes are Markdown files in Obsidian. You read them in Obsidian; the agent reads and writes them in the folder. Both see the same files. Give each claim a source page number so you can check it at any time.

Exercise 1: get fourteen full texts, 15 minutes

Read papers.md. For papers on arXiv, download PDFs through the arXiv API. For papers only on publisher sites, open their DOIs in Ego and download the full texts.
Save everything to vault/sources/, using the first author's surname and the year as the filename.
Check two things for each PDF: it has more than zero pages, and words from the paper's title appear on the first or second page. If it doesn't match, say what you downloaded instead.
Write the results to vault/sources/manifest.csv: ID, title, source, filename, page count, and whether the title check passed.
Stop and call me at human verification; you can accept agreement and consent pages.

The two publisher papers are the focus here: watch the Ego window as the agent opens the library login and finds the download button.

Exercise 2: read in parallel and build a knowledge base, 20 minutes

Split the fourteen papers in vault/sources/ into four groups, one per Sub-agent.
Each Sub-agent reads only its assigned papers and uses vault/templates/paper.md to write one page of notes per paper to vault/papers/:
the question, experimental setting, main results with numbers, a source page number for each claim, and one sentence on how it relates to our topic.
Sub-agents return only the list of files they wrote, without pasting the notes back.
Once all are done, read all the notes and create three to five concept pages in vault/concepts/. Each has one overview paragraph, then a list of what the relevant papers say, with [[wikilinks]] back to the paper pages.
Also write vault/conflicts.md: which pairs of papers reach opposite conclusions under which settings, with each one's numbers and page numbers.

When it is done, pick two notes pages in Obsidian, turn to the cited pages in the papers, and check the numbers.

Exercise 3: propose three testable questions, 10 minutes

Start a new conversation. Give it only the knowledge base, not your earlier conversation:

Read only the concept pages and conflicts.md under vault/, and the experiment code and results in ../lesson-1/. Read nothing else.
Propose three research questions. Each must be testable by changing a few lines in ../lesson-1/code/run_experiment.py and running it on a laptop within an hour.
Write each as a paper title and an abstract. In the abstract, give predicted results and numbers, and say what result would refute it.
On the last line, name the notes pages it came from. Save to vault/questions.md.

Then check novelty in Codex:

Read vault/questions.md and vault/papers/. For each question, judge whether a paper in the knowledge base has already answered it.
Then search OpenAlex by keywords and list the three closest papers and how they differ from this question. Don't change the questions. Write your conclusions to vault/novelty.md.

If a question already has an answer, return to Claude and replace it. In Session 3 you will pick one of the three remaining questions and actually run it.

Where agents go wrong most

The downloaded file is not the paper. A publisher’s generic download link sometimes returns a copyright form, submission checklist, or even another paper from the references. A slow site may return a broken file with zero pages. The filename is right but the content is wrong. Check each PDF’s page count and first-page title. If they don’t match, try a preprint, the author’s site or an institutional repository.

Treating consent as a CAPTCHA, or the reverse. The agent can accept privacy, cookie and authorization consent pages during login. Human verification and image CAPTCHAs need you. State this division in the task card so it does not stop at every page or try to force its way through a CAPTCHA.

Reading dozens of papers in one conversation. By paper twenty, the first ten are hazy, and concept pages start attributing numbers to the wrong papers. Have Sub-agents read groups of papers and save notes to files. The main conversation reads only the notes.

Notes without page numbers. A claim without a page number cannot be checked. A month later, even you will hesitate to use it. Make page numbers a required template field.

Questions come from the conversation instead of the literature. If you discussed your ideas in the same conversation, the agent’s questions drift toward them. They look good, but they are things you already said. Propose questions in a new conversation that reads only the knowledge base, and name the notes pages each question came from.

The list is too narrow. Ask for only the five most relevant papers at the start, and later you find a whole line of work missing. Filling gaps is harder than removing extras. Start broad and cut after reading.

Inside the AI Scientist. The AI Scientist stores papers it has read as a literature forest. Each paper is broken into settings, results and claims. With a time cutoff, it uses earlier papers to predict later results; only insights that predict correctly count as learned. This session’s knowledge base is a small version of that. The rule that questions come from literature rather than conversation comes from there too.

Now with your own direction

Pick a small direction you know. Have the agent use OpenAlex to find the twenty most-cited papers from the last three years. Remove the irrelevant ones, then repeat exercises 1 to 3. Use your own judgment to assess its three questions: which would you give a student, and why?
