6.3 原点漂移与迁移

学习目标

  1. 完整记录原点身份，用锚定任务诊断原点漂移，并把原点、任务与评审的变化分开。
  2. 设计迁移矩阵，以各原点自身的无经验与安慰剂基线判断经验是否迁移，并推导漂移对增量的影响界。
  3. 针对经验迁移提出可检验的竞争预测，在固定尺度下比较经验学习与参数学习。

课后深入专题，约 60 分钟。

一张经验榜上周有效，本周收益消失。原因可能是原点升级，也可能是任务改变或评审尺度改变。三种变化同时发生时，单看一条总分曲线无法区分。

原点的完整记录

闭源 Agent 包括模型与厂商的执行循环，还依赖可见的工具和权限。应记录产品版本与模型标识，注明可选参数、工具清单与执行时间，并保存能够取得的服务端信息。

厂商未公开的版本无法强行固定。可用一组不参与学习的锚定任务定期测量无经验表现，观测到变化后分段统计。锚定集只用于诊断漂移，不能反复用于挑选经验。

迁移矩阵

在原点 A 上学出 BAB_A，在原点 B 上学出 BBB_B，两份榜都冻结，再分别在两个原点上评估：

经验来源 原点 A 执行 原点 B 执行
A 学出的榜 JA(BA)J_A(B_A) JB(BA)J_B(B_A)
B 学出的榜 JA(BB)J_A(B_B) JB(BB)J_B(B_B)

每个执行原点都另设无经验组和匹配安慰剂组。迁移以相对本原点基线的增量来衡量，避免把原点 B 本身更强误读为经验榜迁移成功。

源榜收益接近零时，迁移率这一比值会很不稳定。应优先报告各格的差值和区间，比值只作为有适用条件的辅助量。

漂移对收益的影响界

固定任务分布、预算与评审，对所考虑的经验集合定义：

D=supB|Jv′(B)−Jv(B)|.D=\sup_B|J_{v'}(B)-J_v(B)|.

令某张榜的无经验增量为 Δv(B)=Jv(B)−Jv(⌀)\Delta_v(B)=J_v(B)-J_v(\varnothing)，由三角不等式得：

|Δv′(B)−Δv(B)|≤2D.|\Delta_{v'}(B)-\Delta_v(B)|\le 2D.

这条界表明，原点表现的变化会同时进入有榜与无榜两项。实际只能测量有限张榜，得到的是这些榜上的漂移读数，不能把样本中的最大差直接当作整个经验空间的已知上界。

经验迁移的可检验预测

可以提出三条相互竞争的预测：通用操作经验被更强的原点吸收，领域新规律仍有增量，绑定特定工具行为的经验随接口变化而失效。

先按经验来源与动作类型分层，再比较新旧原点上的条目消融结果。原点更强，并不意味着每条经验的收益必然降低；新能力也可能使原先无法执行的经验变得可用。

因此可以检验这样一条预测：原点越强，通用经验的收益越低。用同一张榜、共同任务和一致的评审测量新旧版本，并加入训练截止之后的新知识条目作为比较。

固定尺度的跨范式比较

要比较闭源原点外的经验学习与开放模型的参数学习，可采用二维设计：一个维度是模型来源，另一个维度是学习方式。开放模型也加入经验榜，才能把原点能力与更新方式的作用分开。

公平性至少要报告两套预算：相同的调用或采样次数，以及相同的总资源成本。参数训练的预算包含训练开销；经验学习的预算包括写榜与检索、搜索与评估。两套结果一并报告，研究主张由这一完整比较支撑。

练习

用包内的演算数据，比较同一张榜在两种原点记录下的内容差与总效应。依据两次再评估的差异，确定需要重新测试的条目。

再为自己的研究设计迁移矩阵，写明哪一格用来检验收益是否保持，哪一格用来分离原点能力，哪项观测会要求重新学习经验榜。

常见错误与处理方法

新原点总分更高，即宣布旧榜有效。 比较该原点自身无经验组与安慰剂组基础上的增量。

经验榜、任务和评审同时更新。 冻结其中两项，用桥接比较检验第三项，各项变化分别记录身份。

迁移比值的分母很小，仍强调倍数。 先报告绝对差和不确定性，再解释比例。

可对照 SkillOpt 的技能优化，逐项比较原点边界、迁移设置和成本口径。

6.3 Origin drift and transfer

Learning outcomes

After this section, students should be able to:

  1. Record an origin as a complete system and use anchor tasks to detect drift without contaminating the selection of experience.
  2. Design and interpret a transfer matrix that measures increments over each origin’s own baselines, and derive the bound that origin drift places on a learning gain.
  3. Formulate testable predictions about which kinds of experience retain value across origins, and design fair comparisons across learning paradigms at a fixed budget.

Extension reading and exercises, about 60 minutes.

A playbook that helped last week may show no gain this week. The origin may have been upgraded, the tasks may have changed, or the scoring criteria may have changed. When all three change together, a single curve of total scores cannot separate them.

Recording the origin as a whole

A closed-source agent comprises the model and the vendor’s execution loop, and also depends on the tools and permissions available to it. Record the product version, model identifier, optional parameters, tool list and time of execution, and save whatever server-side information is available.

A version that the vendor does not expose cannot be pinned. A set of anchor tasks that take no part in learning is used to measure no-playbook performance at regular intervals, and once a change is observed, statistics are computed by segment. The anchor set serves drift diagnosis and must not be used repeatedly to select experience.

Constructing a transfer matrix

Learn BAB_A on origin A and BBB_B on origin B, and freeze both playbooks. Evaluate each on both origins:

Source of experience Run on origin A Run on origin B
Playbook learned on A JA(BA)J_A(B_A) JB(BA)J_B(B_A)
Playbook learned on B JA(BB)J_A(B_B) JB(BB)J_B(B_B)

Each executing origin also has its own no-playbook and matched-placebo groups. Transfer is measured as the increment over that origin’s own baseline, so that a generally stronger origin B is not mistaken for successful transfer of the playbook.

When the source playbook’s gain is close to zero, a transfer ratio becomes very unstable. Report the difference and interval in each cell first, and treat the ratio as an auxiliary quantity with stated conditions of use.

The effect of drift on gains can be bounded

Fix the task distribution, budget and reviewer, and for the set of experience under consideration define:

D=supB|Jv′(B)−Jv(B)|.D=\sup_B|J_{v'}(B)-J_v(B)|.

Let a playbook’s increment over no playbook be Δv(B)=Jv(B)−Jv(⌀)\Delta_v(B)=J_v(B)-J_v(\varnothing). By the triangle inequality:

|Δv′(B)−Δv(B)|≤2D.|\Delta_{v'}(B)-\Delta_v(B)|\le 2D.

The bound expresses the fact that a change in origin performance enters both the with-playbook and the no-playbook terms. In practice only a finite number of playbooks are measured, which gives an estimate of drift on those playbooks; the largest difference in the sample cannot be taken as a known upper bound for the whole space of experience.

Testing which experience retains its value

Three competing predictions can be stated: general operating experience is absorbed by stronger origins, new regularities in a domain still add value, and experience tied to particular tool behavior ceases to work when the interface changes.

First stratify by source of experience and type of action, then examine entry ablations on the old and new origins. A stronger origin does not mathematically force every entry to lose value; new capabilities may also make previously unusable experience actionable.

The statement that general experience is worth less as the origin becomes stronger is therefore treated as a testable prediction. Test the old and new versions with the same playbook, shared tasks and a consistent reviewer, and then add entries containing new knowledge from after the training cutoff for comparison.

Cross-paradigm comparison at a fixed scale

To compare experience learning outside a closed-source origin with parameter learning in an open model, a two-factor design can be used, with model source on one axis and learning method on the other. Only when the open model also receives a playbook can the effect of origin capability be separated from that of the update method.

For fairness, report at least two budgets: the same number of calls or samples, and the same total resource cost. Parameter training includes the training overhead; experience learning includes writing the playbook, retrieval, search and evaluation. Both sets of results are reported together, and the full comparison is used to support the research claim.

Exercise

Using the worked-example data in the package, compare the content difference and total effect of the same playbook under the two origin records. Use the difference between the two re-evaluations to identify the entries that require retesting.

Then design your own transfer matrix, stating which cell tests whether value is retained, which cell separates origin capability, and which observation would require the playbook to be learned again.

Common errors and corrections

Concluding that the old playbook is useful because the new origin’s total score is higher. Compare the increments over that origin’s own no-playbook and placebo groups.

Updating the playbook, the tasks and the reviewer at once. Freeze two of them, use bridging tasks to compare the third, and give each change its own identity.

Emphasizing a transfer ratio whose denominator is very small. Report the absolute difference and its uncertainty first, then interpret the ratio.

Compare the skill optimization in SkillOpt, then review the origin boundary, the transfer setting and the cost accounting item by item.
