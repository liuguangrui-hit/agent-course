3.3 结果判读

课后深入，约 80 分钟。

带噪验证早停比干净验证早停高 0.99 个百分点。你准备怎样写这句结论？先看差值的不确定性，再看实验是否区分了你关心的解释。

配对差值与区间估计

设同一种子下两种方法的差值为 did_i，共 nn 个独立种子。报告配对均值与区间：

d‾=1n∑idi,d‾±tn−1,0.975sdn. \bar d=\frac{1}{n}\sum_i d_i,\qquad \bar d\pm t_{n-1,0.975}\frac{s_d}{\sqrt n}.

预览的 CE 四成噪声比较，五个种子的差值标准差约 1.07 个百分点，得到 95% 区间负 0.34 到 2.32。这里的区间来自配对差值，不能用两组各自的标准差替代。

种子数很少时，区间依赖差值分布假设。把每个差值也展示出来，检查异常值与依赖；任务或数据集也有抽样时，按真正独立的单位做分层分析。

差异检验与等价检验

区间跨零，当前数据还无法确认正负差异。若研究主张是两种验证方式在实际应用上等价，先给有意义的界，例如正负 2 个百分点，再做等价检验和样本量设计。

标准的双单侧等价检验在每侧 5% 水平下，看 90% 区间是否完全落在等价界内。95% 区间完全落入是更保守的充分条件。预览的 95% 区间超过正 2，单凭它不能宣布这个等价主张成立。

想证明事前提出的等价主张，增加独立重复并降低估计不确定性，保留原先的界。根据结果临时放宽界，会改变要回答的问题。

带噪验证标签为什么仍能排序

先看一个可以推导的情形。CC 类分类中，验证标签以概率 η\eta 均匀翻成其他类别，且翻转独立于输入和预测。某个固定模型的干净验证准确率是 aa，带噪准确率的期望为：

ã=(1−η)a+ηC−1(1−a)=(1−CηC−1)a+ηC−1. \tilde a=(1-\eta)a+\frac{\eta}{C-1}(1-a) =\left(1-\frac{C\eta}{C-1}\right)a+\frac{\eta}{C-1}.

当 η<(C−1)/C\eta<(C-1)/C，斜率为正，期望上的模型排名保持不变。十分类、四成翻转时，斜率约 0.556。标签变坏了，平均排序信息仍能留下来，但有限验证集上的噪声会让排序更不稳定。

练习包的 verify_noise_identity.py，枚举正确与错误预测下的翻转结果，复算这条等式。再读原实验的早停实现：它监测的是交叉熵，这条准确率等式并没有直接证明该实现的行为。下一步可以比较两种验证指标，再改变噪声生成方式。

多次尝试的完整记录

试十种设置只展示最好的一种，会把随机波动变成方法优势。计划中指定主要比较；其余作为探索性分析保留全部尝试，新的发现再用独立数据或新种子确认。

3.3 Interpreting results

After-class deep dive, about 80 minutes.

Accuracy with early stopping on noisy validation is 0.99 percentage points higher than with early stopping on clean validation. How will you write that conclusion? First look at the uncertainty of the difference, then at whether the experiment separated the explanations you care about.

Estimate paired differences and intervals

Let the difference between the two methods under the same seed be did_i, over nn independent seeds. Report the paired mean and interval:

d‾=1n∑idi,d‾±tn−1,0.975sdn. \bar d=\frac{1}{n}\sum_i d_i,\qquad \bar d\pm t_{n-1,0.975}\frac{s_d}{\sqrt n}.

In the preview’s CE comparison at 40% noise, the standard deviation of the differences over five seeds is about 1.07 percentage points, giving a 95% interval of minus 0.34 to 2.32. This interval comes from the paired differences; it cannot be replaced by each group’s own standard deviation.

With very few seeds, the interval depends on assumptions about the distribution of differences. Show each difference as well, and check for outliers and dependence; when tasks or datasets are also sampled, do a stratified analysis by the truly independent unit.

Distinguish tests of difference from tests of equivalence

When the interval crosses zero, the current data cannot yet confirm a positive or negative difference. If the research claim is that the two validation approaches are equivalent in practice, first give a meaningful margin, such as plus or minus 2 percentage points, then do an equivalence test and sample-size planning.

The standard two one-sided tests procedure, at the 5% level on each side, checks whether the 90% interval lies entirely within the equivalence margin. A 95% interval lying entirely inside is a more conservative sufficient condition. The preview’s 95% interval goes beyond plus 2, so on its own it cannot establish this equivalence claim.

To prove an equivalence claim stated in advance, add independent replications and reduce the uncertainty of the estimate, keeping the original margin. Widening the margin on the spot in light of the result changes the question being answered.

Why noisy validation labels can preserve model rankings

Start with a case we can analyze mathematically. In CC-class classification, validation labels are flipped uniformly to the other classes with probability η\eta, independently of the input and the prediction. If a fixed model’s clean validation accuracy is aa, the expected noisy accuracy is:

ã=(1−η)a+ηC−1(1−a)=(1−CηC−1)a+ηC−1. \tilde a=(1-\eta)a+\frac{\eta}{C-1}(1-a) =\left(1-\frac{C\eta}{C-1}\right)a+\frac{\eta}{C-1}.

When η<(C−1)/C\eta<(C-1)/C, the slope is positive and the expected ranking of models stays the same. With ten classes and 40% flips, the slope is about 0.556. The labels got worse, but the average ranking information survives; noise on a finite validation set still makes the ranking less stable.

The exercise package’s verify_noise_identity.py enumerates the flip outcomes under correct and incorrect predictions and recomputes this identity. Then read the early-stopping implementation of the original experiment: it monitors cross-entropy, and this accuracy identity does not directly prove how that implementation behaves. A next step is to compare the two validation metrics, then change how the noise is generated.

Design controls that distinguish mechanisms

Competing explanation New intervention What to look at
Validation labels still keep ranking information Raise validation noise step by step, with the training trajectory fixed How the quality of the chosen epoch and the rank correlation decline
Stopping early is already enough by itself Draw random stopping epochs in advance, matching the average training budget Whether validation-based selection beats random selection
The symmetric noise structure preserved the signal Switch to class-dependent or instance-dependent noise Whether the advantage in ranking and epoch selection changes

First record each explanation’s prediction in advance, then run, and decide the next experiment from the observations in the table.

Record all experimental attempts

Trying ten settings and showing only the best turns random fluctuation into a method advantage. Name the main comparison in the plan; keep all other attempts as exploratory analysis, and confirm new findings with independent data or new seeds.

When an effect depends on a setting, first ask which mechanism predicted that dependence. Add cross-condition experiments and report the interaction, rather than sorting results into significant and not significant.

Exercise

Run the identity check in the package, then propose a case where cross-entropy early stopping and accuracy-based epoch selection might differ. State which additional curve you would record, how to analyze it in pairs, and which result would change the explanation. You can compare selection rules on the same training trajectory; the test set is used only for the final report.

Common mistakes and how to fix them

Treating the peak of the test curve as an achievable algorithm. It can describe the trajectory or serve as a post-hoc reference, but the actual selection must use only information allowed in advance.

Letting the derivation of the expected ranking replace a finite-sample experiment. Add controls on validation-set size and noise type, and measure ranking stability.

Switching to a significant result when the main comparison fails. Keep the conclusion of the main comparison, and confirm exploratory findings with the next independent experiment.
