1 Agent 写作

一小时后，你手里有一篇从题目到结论整体重讲过的论文，两张能直接放进投稿的数据图，一份另一家 Agent 写的编辑审稿意见，还有一个下次改论文时一句话就能调出的 Skill。

课堂核心 60 分钟。课后深入 1.1 至 1.6 共约三个半小时，按需选读。

下载第 1 课练习材料

练习用的是一篇四页的短论文：几种训练损失在训练标签有错时表现如何。实验是真的，code/run_experiment.py 在笔记本上一分钟就能重跑出全部数据；文字和图则是常见的初稿样子。

先看成品，5 分钟

左边的题目三行长，摘要从深度学习的成功讲起，最重要的结果藏在第七句，前面还挂着 may 和 approximately。右边的题目是一句论断，摘要第一句就是最抓人的现象：标签错四成时，网络第 3 轮已经学到 92%，之后 77 轮都在记错误标签，最后掉到 67%。

左边二十条线缠在一起，图例盖住数据，颜色循环用完后重复，看不出哪条是哪个方法。右边每个方法一条均值线加误差带，名字直接标在线旁，按单栏宽度原尺寸画，放进论文后字号仍有 7 磅。

两版用的是同一份数据，没有新增一个实验。

讲，10 分钟

论文靠一个点抓人。 审稿人先读题目和摘要，决定要不要往下读。题目是招牌，可以说大。摘要第一句给最抓人的结果，接着按读者会追问的顺序展开：它成立吗，为什么，换个条件还成立吗。结果节也按这个顺序排，每段第一句写结论，局限在讨论里集中讲一次。

Agent 的初稿为什么总有 AI 味。 它天生谨慎，每句都留后路，同一句限定在摘要和结论里各说一遍。让它一句一句地润色，这些只会越改越多。管用的是给一篇目标刊会的范文，让它读懂全文后合上原稿，围绕最抓人的一点另起一稿重讲。

图按最终尺寸画，在 PDF 里看。 先想好这张图要读者看到哪一个结论，再按栏宽直接画矢量图。画完让 Agent 看编译出来的页面，很多问题只有放进栏里才看得出来。

写和审分给两家。 同一个模型写完再审，品味相同，问题互相看不见。这堂课让 Claude 写，让 Codex 以编辑的眼光审。

然后给出三个题目，每个不超过十个词，要像范文那样是一句论断。先停下来等我选。

挑一个题目，或者自己改一个，然后：

用我选的题目。不要在 paper/main.tex 上改，合上原稿，照范文的讲法另起一稿写全文，存到 versions/v1/main.tex。
摘要不超过 150 词，第一句就是最抓人的结果，每个发现只带一个数字。
引言第一段讲那个最抓人的例子；结果按读者会追问的顺序排，每段第一句是结论；局限只在讨论里讲一段。

初稿里埋了两处数字错误。重讲之后它们还在不在，靠读是读不出来的，1.2 数字从数据来讲怎样用脚本把每个数字对回数据。

练习 2：把数据图画到能投稿，15 分钟

读 code/plot_draft.py 和 style_table.md 的图部分，重写一个 code/plot_paper.py：
按单栏宽度 3.25 英寸原尺寸画，字号 7 到 9 磅，输出矢量 PDF 到 versions/v1/figures/；
每个方法在两张图里用同一种色盲友好的颜色；多个种子画均值线加一个标准差的带；
线旁直接标方法名，不用盖住数据的图例；图里不放标题。
每张图先用一句话告诉我它要读者看到什么结论，再动手画，并把这句话写成图注的第一句。
改 versions/v1/main.tex 引用新图，编译后把 PDF 的每一页渲染成图片，自己检查有没有文字重叠、出界或者字太小，有问题就改。

最后一句很关键。Agent 能看图片，让它看编译后的页面，而不是只看单独导出的图。

最容易出错的地方

让它随手装到系统里。 不加限制，Agent 可能用管理员权限装包、改系统自带的 Python，几个项目从此互相打架。要求它每个项目建独立环境，装之前先报清单，等你点头。

逐句修补，越改越长。 每轮改动都在原句上加一句补充或限定，几轮下来比初稿还长，重点被淹没。改不动的时候，让它合上原稿整体重讲；就算是按审稿意见修改，也是重讲。

叮嘱它要严谨。 写作任务里加一句不要夸大、保持原有结论范围，结果就是满篇 may 和 to some extent。写作任务只交代读者和最想让读者记住什么，数字对不对交给核对脚本。

让 Agent 读一遍说数字没问题。 它会认真地读，然后告诉你一致。初稿里摘要写 96.2%，表里是 96.0，这种差别读不出来，只能用脚本对回数据。见 1.2。

图在论文里字只有 4 磅。 按默认的 8 乘 6 英寸画好再缩进 3.25 英寸的栏，字就缩到原来的四成。按最终尺寸画，在编译后的 PDF 里检查。见 1.3。

自己审自己。 同一个模型写完再审，挑出的多是错别字。换一家的 Agent 审，意见注明审的是哪一版；稿子改了就重审，旧意见不能挂在新稿上。见 1.4。

在 AI 科学家里。 AI 科学家写论文也是这几步：叙事编辑读懂全文后照目标刊会的范文另起一稿，再由另一家厂商的 Agent 以编辑的眼光审稿，读完题目和摘要不想往下读就退回重写，连续三次不过才交给人裁定。本课每一条容易出错的地方，都是它在真实论文上踩过的坑。

1 Writing with Agents

In an hour you will have a paper retold from title to conclusion, two data figures that can go straight into a submission, an editorial review written by a second vendor’s agent, and a Skill that brings the whole method back with one line the next time you revise a paper.

Core class: 60 minutes. Self-study sections 1.1 to 1.6 take about three and a half hours in total; read what you need.

Download the Session 1 materials

The exercise paper is a four-page study of how training losses cope when some training labels are wrong. The experiment is real: code/run_experiment.py regenerates all the data in about a minute on a laptop. The text and figures look like a typical first draft.

Install tools at the start: send the card in 1 minute

This session’s hidden thread: let the agent compile, draw figures and look at pages. Session 0 used only the agent’s ability to read and write text. Revising papers needs three more things: a Python environment to run experiments and draw figures, LaTeX to compile, and PDF rendering tools to turn compiled pages into images so the agent can check layout itself. Give installation to the agent. It installs in the background while you keep listening.

Open the lesson-1 folder in Claude, set permissions to Accept edits, and send this task card first:

Before installing, list what you plan to install, where, and roughly how large it is. Wait for my OK.

Finally add two lines to AGENTS.md: how to compile this project and how to render its pages.

See the finished product, 5 minutes

On the left the title runs three lines, the abstract opens with the success of deep learning, and the key result hides in the seventh sentence behind “may” and “approximately”. On the right the title is a claim, and the abstract’s first sentence is the most striking observation: with 40% of labels wrong, the network reaches 92% by epoch 3, spends the next 77 epochs memorizing the wrong labels, and ends at 67%.

On the left twenty lines tangle together, the legend covers the data, and the color cycle runs out and repeats, so you cannot tell which line belongs to which method. On the right each method is one mean line with an error band, its name sits next to the line, and the figure is drawn at its final one-column width, so the text is still 7 pt once it is in the paper.

Talk, 10 minutes

A paper hooks the reader with one point. Reviewers read the title and abstract first and decide whether to read on. The title is a sign, and it can claim big. The abstract’s first sentence gives the most striking result, then follows the questions a reader would ask in order: does it hold, why, does it still hold under other conditions. The results section follows the same order, each paragraph opening with its conclusion, and the limitations are stated once, together, in the discussion.

Why agent drafts always smell of AI. Agents are cautious by nature. Every sentence gets an escape hatch, and the same caveat turns up in both the abstract and the conclusion. Ask one to polish sentence by sentence and these only multiply. What works is to give it an exemplar from the target venue, have it read and understand the whole paper, then set the draft aside and retell it from scratch around the single most striking point.

Draw figures at final size and look at them in the PDF. First decide which one conclusion the figure should show the reader, then draw it as a vector graphic at column width. When it is done, have the agent look at the compiled page. Many problems only show once the figure sits in the column.

Split writing and reviewing between two vendors. A model that reviews what it just wrote shares its own taste, so the problems stay invisible to it. In this session Claude writes and Codex reviews with an editor’s eye.

Exercise 1: retell the whole paper, 20 minutes

Open the lesson-1 folder in Claude:

Read paper/main.tex and the two data files in data/.
Then download the PDF of arXiv 1611.03530 into exemplar/ and read its title, abstract and the first paragraph of its introduction.
Tell me how the exemplar's opening hooks the reader, and what the single most striking result in our paper is, with its number.
Then propose three titles, each ten words or fewer, each a claim the way the exemplar's title is. Stop and wait for me to choose.

Pick a title, or adjust one yourself, then:

Use the title I chose. Don't edit paper/main.tex. Set the draft aside and write the whole paper afresh, told the way the exemplar tells things, and save it to versions/v1/main.tex.
The abstract is at most 150 words, its first sentence is the most striking result, and each finding carries one number only.
The first paragraph of the introduction tells the most striking example. Order the results by the questions a reader would ask, each paragraph opening with its conclusion. Limitations get one paragraph, in the discussion.
Keep the figures in paper/figures/ for now. Compile the PDF and tell me how many pages it has.

The draft hides two wrong numbers. You cannot tell by reading whether they survived the retell. 1.2 Numbers come from data shows how to check every number against the data with a script.

Exercise 2: bring the data figures up to submission standard, 15 minutes

Read code/plot_draft.py and the figures part of style_table.md, then write a new code/plot_paper.py:
draw at the final single-column width of 3.25 inches, text 7 to 9 pt, vector PDF output to versions/v1/figures/;
one color-blind-safe color per method, the same in both figures; for multiple seeds, a mean line with a one-standard-deviation band;
label lines directly instead of using a legend that covers the data; no title inside the plot.
Before drawing each figure, tell me in one sentence what conclusion it should show the reader, and make that sentence the first sentence of its caption.

The last instruction matters most. Agents can look at images, so have it look at the compiled page instead of the figure exported on its own.

Where agents go wrong most

Letting it install into the system. Without limits, the agent may install packages with administrator privileges or change the system Python, leaving projects in conflict. Ask for a separate environment per project, an installation list first, and your OK before it proceeds.

Patching sentence by sentence, the paper keeps growing. Each round adds a clarification or a caveat to the sentence already there, and a few rounds later the paper is longer than the draft and the point is buried. When it stops improving, have the agent set the draft aside and retell the whole paper. Revising from review comments is a retell too.

Telling it to be rigorous. Add “don’t overclaim” or “keep the original scope of the conclusions” to a writing task and you get “may” and “to some extent” all over the page. A writing task says only who the reader is and what they should remember most. Leave the correctness of the numbers to a checking script.

Having the agent read the paper and declare the numbers fine. It reads carefully, then tells you everything matches. In the draft the abstract says 96.2% and the table says 96.0. A gap like that does not show up on reading; only a script that checks against the data catches it. See 1.2.

Figures with 4 pt text in the paper. Draw at the default 8 by 6 inches, shrink into a 3.25-inch column, and the text shrinks to 40% of its size. Draw at final size and check in the compiled PDF. See 1.3.

Reviewing its own work. The same model reviewing what it wrote mostly finds typos. Have another vendor’s agent review, and make every review name the version it read. When the paper changes, review again; old comments do not carry over to a new draft. See 1.4.

Inside the AI Scientist. The AI Scientist writes papers in the same steps. A narrative editor reads and understands the whole paper, then writes a fresh draft modeled on an exemplar from the target venue. An agent from a different vendor reviews it with an editor’s eye; if the title and abstract don’t make it want to read on, the paper goes back for a rewrite, and only after three failed rounds does a person decide. Every pitfall in this session is one it has hit on real papers.

  • A paper hooks with one point: the title is a sign, the abstract’s first sentence gives the result, and the results follow the reader’s questions.
  • Revise with an exemplar and a full retell; sentence-by-sentence patching only adds length. Leave the numbers to a checking script.
  • Draw figures at final size and check them in the compiled PDF.
  • Give writing and reviewing to two vendors’ agents, and tie every review to a specific version.
