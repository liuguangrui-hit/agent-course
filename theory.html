6 选修 编排理论

一小时后，你有一本用量账本，记着工作台里每件事花了多少 token 和钱，用了多长时间，结果有没有被采用；你能用它算出三个编排决策：一个对话该在什么时候结束，审稿该在第几轮停，一件事该交给一个 Agent 慢慢做还是拆给几个并行做。

课堂核心 60 分钟。课后深入 6.1 约 30 分钟。第 4 课之后选修。

下载第 6 课练习材料

练习材料里有一份示例账本 ledger.csv，按一个工作台两周的典型用法编成，四十件事；calc.py 是三个决策的计算骨架。你也可以直接用自己第 4 课工作台的记录。

开课先装工具，1 分钟发出去

本课暗线：给工作台装一本账。 前几课装的工具都是让 Agent 多做事，这一课装的工具让你知道每件事花了多少。没有账，所有编排上的讨论都只能凭感觉：要不要让另一家审，要不要开 Sub-agent，审几轮。有了账，这些都可以算。

这个项目要记用量账。装 ccusage，用它列出最近两周每个对话的 token 用量、估算费用和起止时间。
然后建 ledger.csv，每件事一行：日期，事情，用的 Agent，对话数，token，估算费用，耗时，结果是否被采用。
能从 ccusage 里对上的自动填，对不上的列出来问我。装之前告诉我装什么，等我同意。

先看成品，5 分钟

打开 preview/decisions.md，这是 calc.py 在示例账本上算出来的。三段，每段一个决策，一张小表，一个数字。第一段：几条六十到一百轮的长对话，每 27 轮左右把进度写进文件，开新对话，总成本省下四成多。第二段：审稿第一轮总能提出被采纳的实质意见，第二轮一半，第三轮只剩八分之一，按一条意见值 50 万 token 算，第三轮不值得开。第三段：读 14 篇论文拆给四个 Sub-agent 后，耗时从 50 分钟降到 14 分钟，token 多花了 18%。

讲，10 分钟

一个对话的成本随长度平方增长。 Agent 每回答一次，都把对话里此前的全部内容重读一遍。如果每轮新增的内容差不多，第 k 轮要读 k 份，n 轮一共读 1 + 2 + … + n，约 n² / 2 份。对话长度翻倍，成本变成四倍。缓存能打折扣，但不改变平方的形状。这是一个简化模型，真实的增长要拿你自己的日志来验，练习 1 就做这件事。这就是第 0 课说一件事一个对话的道理，现在可以算出来：把一个 60 轮的对话拆成三个 20 轮的对话，只要交接文件不大，重读的总量大约降到原来的三分之一。

再做一轮的价值，要和它的成本比。 审稿每多一轮，都有一定概率发现一个真问题。这个概率一轮比一轮小。当某一轮发现问题的概率乘以一个问题的价值，小于这一轮的花费，就该停了。概率不用猜，从账本里过去的审稿记录数出来。

并行主要买的是时间。 把一件事拆给四个 Sub-agent，每个都要读一遍任务说明，主对话还要合并，token 总量常常不降反升，你的账本会告诉你实际如何。省下来的是墙上的时间。值不值，看你的时间值多少：开会前要结果，并行；夜里跑，不急，串行。

下一个问题挑哪个，是探索和利用的平衡。 第 4 课的循环每晚挑一个问题。总挑最有把握的，只会在一个小角落里打转；总挑最新的，什么都做不深。把每类问题的预测命中率和信息量记在账上，大部分时间挑期望收获最大的，留一小部分给还没试过的方向。

练习 1：一个对话该在何时结束，15 分钟

Claude Code 的原始对话日志里，每次回答都记着这一次的输入 token。取最长的三个对话，按回答的先后画输入 token 随轮次变化的曲线。
对照 6.1 的分段算式估算：如果每 20 轮把进度写进一个 2 千 token 的文件、开新对话，总成本能省多少。
把计算过程和结论写进 decisions.md 第一段。

练习 2：审稿该在第几轮停，15 分钟

读 ledger.csv 里所有审稿的记录，按 review_round 和 substantive 两列，以及对应的 review.md。数出每一轮审稿中，提出过至少一条被采纳的实质意见的比例。
设一条实质意见的价值相当于 V 个 token，你先按我给的 V 等于 50 万算，再告诉我 V 取多少时结论会变。
算出从第几轮开始，期望价值低于这一轮的花费。写进 decisions.md 第二段。

V 是你的判断：一条把论文从被拒拉回来的意见值多少。理论在这里不替你做决定，它把决定拆成一个你能回答的问题。

练习 3：并行还是串行，10 分钟

在 ledger.csv 里找出读论文写笔记的两次记录，一次串行，一次用了 Sub-agent。
比较 token、费用和耗时。按我的时间每小时值 X 元，算出并行在什么条件下划算。写进 decisions.md 第三段。

最容易出错的地方

凭感觉调编排。 觉得多审一轮更稳，觉得开 Sub-agent 更省，结果正相反。先记账，再改编排。

只看 token，不看采用率。 一个流程很省，但产出一半被你推翻重来，实际更贵。账本里要有结果是否被采用这一列，比较的是每个被采用的结果花了多少。

账记成一大笔。 一周的用量只有一个总数，分不出是哪件事花的。按事情分段记，开工和收工各记一次。

拿模型的自我评估当数据。 让 Agent 估计这轮审稿有没有用，它多半说有用。采纳与否由你或者后续结果判定，不让做事的那个 Agent 自己打分。

在 AI 科学家里。 AI 科学家的每个模块开工和收工都记一笔用量，按账号，模块和任务分开。哪个环节交给哪家 Agent，多少事并行，审几轮，都是从这本账里算出来再定的。

6 Elective: Orchestration Theory

In an hour you will have a usage ledger recording each workbench task’s tokens, cost, time and whether its result was used. You can use it to calculate three orchestration decisions: when to end a conversation, which review round to stop at, and whether one agent should do a task in sequence or several should do it in parallel.

Core class: 60 minutes. Self-study section 6.1 takes about 30 minutes. An elective after Session 4.

Download the Session 6 materials

The materials include an example ledger.csv with forty tasks, constructed around two weeks of typical workbench use. calc.py is a calculation skeleton for the three decisions. You can also use records from your own Session 4 workbench.

Install tools at the start: send the card in 1 minute

This session’s hidden thread: give the workbench a ledger. Earlier tools let the agent do more. This one tells you what each task costs. Without records, orchestration decisions are guesswork: another vendor’s review, Sub-agents, how many review rounds. With records, you can calculate them.

This project needs a usage ledger. Install ccusage and use it to list each conversation's token usage, estimated cost, and start and end times for the last two weeks.
Then create ledger.csv, one row per task: date, task, agent used, conversation count, tokens, estimated cost, time taken, and whether the result was used.
Fill what you can match from ccusage automatically. List unmatched items and ask me. Before installing, tell me what you will install and wait for my OK.

See the finished product, 5 minutes

Open preview/decisions.md, calculated by calc.py from the example ledger. Three parts, each with a decision, a small table and a number. First: for several long conversations of sixty to a hundred rounds, writing progress to a file and starting a new conversation about every 27 rounds saves over 40% of the total cost. Second: the first review round always proposes substantive comments that are adopted, the second does so half the time, and the third only one eighth of the time. At a value of 500,000 tokens per comment, round three is not worth starting. Third: splitting the reading of 14 papers among four Sub-agents cut time from 50 minutes to 14, using 18% more tokens.

Talk, 10 minutes

Conversation cost grows with the square of its length. Each response rereads everything earlier in the conversation. If each round adds roughly the same amount, round k reads k units. Across n rounds, that is 1 + 2 + … + n, about n² / 2 units. Double the length and the cost quadruples. Caching discounts the cost without changing the quadratic shape. This is a simplified model; check real growth against your own logs in Exercise 1. It explains Session 0’s one-task-one-conversation rule. You can now calculate it: split a 60-round conversation into three of 20 rounds, and, if the handoff files are small, total rereading falls to about a third.

Compare the value of another round with its cost. Each review round has a chance of finding a real problem, and that chance falls each round. Stop when the chance of finding a problem times its value falls below the round’s cost. Count that chance from past reviews in the ledger rather than guessing.

Parallel work mainly buys time. Split a task among four Sub-agents and each reads the instructions, while the main conversation must merge results. Total tokens often rise rather than fall; your ledger tells you what actually happens. What you save is elapsed time. Whether it is worth it depends on your time’s value: need results before a meeting, parallel; overnight and no hurry, sequential.

Choosing the next question balances exploration and exploitation. Session 4’s loop picks a question each night. Always pick the safest and you circle in a small corner. Always pick the newest and you never go deep. Record prediction accuracy and information gained for each question type. Usually choose the highest expected gain, leaving a small share for untried directions.

Exercise 1: when to end a conversation, 15 minutes

Claude Code's raw conversation logs record input tokens for each response. Take the three longest conversations and plot input tokens against response number.
Use section 6.1's formula for splitting a conversation to estimate savings if, every 20 rounds, you write progress to a 2,000-token file and start a new conversation.
Write the calculation and conclusion as the first part of decisions.md.

Exercise 2: which review round to stop at, 15 minutes

Read all review records in ledger.csv, using the review_round and substantive columns and corresponding review.md files. For each review round, count the share that proposed at least one substantive comment that was adopted.
Let one substantive comment be worth V tokens. First use my V of 500,000, then tell me at what V the conclusion changes.
Calculate the first round whose expected value falls below its cost. Write the second part of decisions.md.

V is your judgment: what is a comment worth if it saves a paper from rejection? Theory does not decide for you here. It turns the decision into a question you can answer.

Exercise 3: parallel or sequential, 10 minutes

Find two records of reading papers and writing notes in ledger.csv: one sequential, one using Sub-agents.
Compare tokens, cost and time. With my time worth X yuan per hour, calculate when parallel work pays off. Write the third part of decisions.md.

Where agents go wrong most

Tuning orchestration by feel. Another review seems safer, Sub-agents seem cheaper, and the result is the opposite. Record usage before changing orchestration.

Counting tokens without adoption. A cheap workflow whose outputs you reject and redo half the time costs more in practice. Include whether the result was used in the ledger. Compare cost per adopted result.

One lump sum. A weekly total cannot tell you which task cost what. Record by task, once at the start and once at the end.

Using the model’s self-assessment as data. Ask the agent whether its review helped and it usually says yes. You or later results judge adoption; the working agent does not score itself.

Inside the AI Scientist. The AI Scientist records usage at each module’s start and finish, separated by account, module and task. Which vendor handles each step, how much runs in parallel and how many review rounds to use are decided after calculating from this ledger.
