4 自动化

本节课目标

完成本节课堂训练及课后练习后，学生应能：

  1. 把一轮研究表达为输入、输出、状态与完成条件明确的状态转移，并区分程序执行失败与科学负结果。
  2. 设计可恢复的执行机制，使重复请求不产生重复效果，并在指定故障点验证恢复行为。
  3. 为调度任务规定执行环境、完成判据与停止条件，依据执行记录核查一次触发的实际结果。
  4. 把运行中的经验整理为带适用条件与来源的候选条目，设计在未见任务上的对照检验，评价其对后续任务的作用。

课堂完成一次故障恢复和一次单次调度；这不足以验证长期运行行为。更换故障位置、少量周期调度和经验的三组检验在课后完成。

课前完成准备，课堂 60 分钟，课后完成复习、独立实践与迁移评价，另可选做深入专题。

课前准备 · 课堂教学 · 课后复习与扩展

本节讨论无人值守研究流程中的两个问题。第一个是恢复：夜间实验已经启动，写入状态时连接中断，之后接手的 Agent 读到的状态是未完成，它可能再启动一份。夜间运行首先要解决的，是恢复时怎样确认已经发生了什么。

第二个是学习：研究循环每天完成一轮，产物不断增加，但系统的能力是否随之提高，需要单独检验。流程衔接完成后，还要为经验设置独立的检验。

课堂教学

一轮研究的记录与交接，5 分钟

先核对 preflight.md，确认本课的本地演练条件，再查看上一轮留下的状态。

练习材料中，LOOP.md 规定交接规则，STATE.md 记录当前步骤，每轮在自己的目录中保存预测、结果与审阅。预览网页由第 3 课的实际运行结果生成，从内部页可以追溯到原始数字。

恢复演示呈现一种故障：产物已经写出，状态尚未提交，程序即退出。再次执行时，程序先核查产物，再补写状态，输出仍然只有一份。课堂据此分析它怎样依据已存在的产物恢复状态。

研究循环的状态转移，10 分钟

一轮研究依次为：选题并冻结预测，执行与校验，审阅并归档。每一次转移都须有完成条件，例如从执行转入校验时，退出码和产物清单都须满足要求。

状态未知时先查询，运行失败时按原因恢复，研究假设被否定时正常进入结论。程序失败与科学负结果分开处理，循环才能判断下一步应修改代码还是修正认识。

自动化固定了这些转移。系统学习则改变以后的选择或做法，并须在未见任务上测得改进。两者在记录中分别呈现。

练习一：中断恢复，15 分钟

先读 recovery_demo.py，说明它模拟的动作与故障点。
按 README 在独立演示目录运行，模拟产物写出后退出，再重新运行。
核对输出数量、事件记录与最终状态，解释它怎样识别同一动作。
随后用同样思路检查 LOOP.md：找出启动实验与写状态之间的空档。

同一动作被重复请求后只产生一次预定效果，称为幂等。一次网络超时并不能说明动作是否已经发生，因此恢复时须能按已知身份查询，或在本地核对对应产物。

练习二：定时审阅与收工交接，15 分钟

沿用第 3 课已完成的一轮，或从包内预览复制为演练起点，标明来源。
把本轮 result.md 交给 wake_review.py，设五秒后触发一次，调用最长九十秒，输出到新的 one-shot-review/。
一起检查 run.json 中的主机与时区、计划时间与实际时间，再核对输入哈希和退出码，并查看最终的 review.md。
只有 completed 才把审阅意见交给新会话；超时、失败和空输出先记录原因，不推进审阅完成状态。
新会话只读 LOOP.md、STATE.md 与本轮记录，核查后接手收工，生成本地页并检查重复效果。

命令由教师和学生共同核对后运行。路径替换为本轮的实际记录，每次使用新的输出目录：

python3 wake_review.py --input preview/runs/1/result.md --out one-shot-review --delay-seconds 5 --timeout-seconds 90

在计划等待期间按 Ctrl+C，再检查 cancelled 与 calls_started 为零，可以验证调用尚未发起。运行期间按 Ctrl+C 会停止本次子进程。最后确认进程已经退出，没有遗留周期唤醒。

任务锁决定哪个会话可以写入，运行身份决定写入对应哪一次动作。锁超时须核查持有者与进程；若旧会话恢复后仍能写入，仅凭时间删除锁会产生两个写入者。

练习三：候选经验及其检验方案，10 分钟

从本轮失败或成功里提炼至多一条经验，写适用条件、具体动作和来源。
列出它预期会改变哪类未见任务，主要评估指标是什么。
保留一份无经验对照，再设计同格式近似长度的安慰剂对照。
经验先标为候选，测试方案放入下一批任务，不把本次成功直接当成有效性证明。

当前循环完成后，课后再设置少量定时运行。先确认停止条件、恢复方法和额度耗尽时的行为，再延长无人值守的时间。

课堂检查与本节小结，5 分钟

常见错误与处理方法

每次恢复都从第一步重做。 状态应指向产物与动作身份，恢复时先核查已经发生的效果。

有锁即认为不存在并发。 核查锁的持有者，以及接管后旧会话能否继续写入；所有共享写入经由同一入口。

轮数增加即宣称系统在进化。 固定未见任务和评分标准，比较是否产生可重复的收益。

丢弃运行失败的记录，结果页只保留成功轮次。 失败轮次保留状态与原因，才能判断改进作用于哪个环节。

本节小结

前几课的文件现在由状态转移连接起来。课堂上的单次定时器按时触发任务，运行器记录执行身份。审阅给出结论判定，经验榜提出下一次的做法。结果页把这些内容汇总到同一入口，供研究者决定下一步工作。

AI 科学家把类似职责分配给独立模块。本课先用一个自己的研究问题跑通最小循环，再考虑扩大任务规模。

课后复习与扩展

概念复习、正反例分析及能力达成评价中的独立实践与迁移任务为本节必做内容；其余深入专题按研究需要选做。

概念复习，15 分钟

只读课堂的 STATE.md 和本轮文件，列出下一位接手者应首先查询的内容。再关闭 Agent，沿状态图走一遍失败恢复，区分程序执行失败和科学负结果。把仍依赖猜测的转移写入 review-after-class.md，并补上可核查的完成条件。

正反例辨析，20 分钟

甲看到状态仍为未启动，直接重新提交实验。乙先用已记录的任务标识查询，发现产物已经生成，只补写状态。分别指出两种做法在断连窗口中可能造成的后果。再看一轮正常执行但否定了预测的实验：它应当重试程序，还是进入结论与经验候选？写出理由。

先画出恢复路径，再查看参考思路

状态落后于实际动作时，甲可能重复执行。乙依据动作身份核查效果，恢复才有依据。程序成功且产物完整的科学负结果可以正常归档；经验是否有效，继续交由未见任务检验。

扩展学习

先重做一次中断恢复，并把故障设在与课堂不同的位置，例如启动成功但回执丢失，或结果页已生成而归档状态未更新。然后从课堂上的单次唤醒出发选择一个专题。周期调度先安排少量运行，核对暂停与删除入口；外部通知和部署沿用已经明确的目标与授权范围。

能力达成评价

评价分三个层次。课堂训练层面，检查学生能否解释恢复演示怎样识别同一动作，单次唤醒的 run.json 是否与计划一致，候选经验是否写明条件、来源和检验方案。课后独立完成层面，学生在另一故障位置重做恢复，说明恢复查询所依据的身份键，并在少量周期运行中演示暂停与停止。迁移层面，学生为自己研究流程中的一个有副作用的步骤画出状态转移，指出状态未知时的查询方式和完成判据，并说明一条经验需要哪些未见任务和对照才能判定有效。评价依据为恢复机制的正确性、停止条件的有效性及经验检验设计。

课后独立实践还须在未见任务上，按匹配预算完成目标经验、匹配安慰剂与无经验三组检验，报告各组结果、效应比较及不确定性，说明适用条件。尚未完成的检验标为待验证；候选经验的作用须依据实际比较结果评价。

4 Research automation

Learning outcomes

After completing this session and its review and extension work, students should be able to:

  1. Represent a research process as a sequence of states with explicit inputs, outputs and completion conditions.
  2. Design execution and recovery mechanisms that determine what has already happened after an interruption and avoid duplicated effects.
  3. Plan scheduled execution with completion criteria, stop conditions and a defined response to failure.
  4. Distinguish program failures from negative scientific results in the record of a research loop.
  5. Evaluate a playbook entry by its effect on unseen tasks against matched controls, rather than by its use in the round that produced it.

The outcomes are assessed at three levels. In class, students observe recovery from one failure point, run one scheduled read-only review with a handover to a new session, and design a test for one candidate playbook entry. These observations do not establish the long-term behavior of the loop. After class, students repeat recovery at a different failure point and extend the schedule to a small number of periodic runs; the extension paths apply the same mechanisms to the evaluation of playbook entries or to the presentation of results.

Preparation before class, a 60-minute class session, then required review, independent work and transfer assessment, with optional further study.

Preparation · Class session · Review and extension

An automated research workflow must be able to determine, when a session resumes, which actions have already taken effect. Consider an experiment launched overnight in which the connection drops while the status is being written. If the next session reads “not finished”, it may start a second copy. The workflow therefore needs a reliable record of state and a recovery procedure that checks effects rather than relying on the last message.

A research loop raises a second question. It may complete one round a day and produce an increasing volume of output without any improvement in capability. Once the process is connected, the methods it records for future use require an independent test.

Class session

Records and handover between rounds, 5 minutes

Confirm from preflight.md that this session requires only a local rehearsal, then examine the state left by the previous round.

In the exercise materials, LOOP.md contains the handover rules and STATE.md records the current step; each round leaves its prediction, result and review in its own directory. The preview pages are generated from the actual runs of Session 3, and the internal page traces back to the raw numbers.

The recovery demonstration presents a failure in which the output has been written, the status has not yet been committed and the program exits. On the next run, the program checks the output first, then completes the status, and the number of outputs remains one. The demonstration shows how the status is recovered from an output that already exists.

Representing the loop as state transitions, 10 minutes

A round of research passes through topic selection, prediction freeze, execution, verification, review and archiving. Every transition requires a completion condition; for example, moving from execution to verification requires that both the exit code and the product list meet their requirements.

An unknown status is queried first, a failed run is recovered according to its cause, and a refuted research hypothesis proceeds normally to the conclusion. Only when program failures and negative scientific results are kept apart can the loop determine whether to correct the code or revise its understanding.

Automation implements these transitions. Learning by the system changes later choices or methods, and any improvement must be measured on unseen tasks. The two are recorded separately.

Exercise 1: recovering from one interruption, 15 minutes

First read recovery_demo.py and explain which action and which failure point it simulates.
Run it in a separate demonstration directory as the README says, simulating an exit after the product is written, then run it again.
Check the number of outputs, the event record and the final state, and explain how it recognizes the same action.
Then use the same idea to check LOOP.md: find the gap between starting the experiment and writing the state.

An action is idempotent when repeated requests for it produce the intended effect only once. A network timeout does not reveal whether the action took place, so recovery must be able to query a known action ID or check the corresponding output locally.

Exercise 2: a scheduled read-only review and handover to a new session, 15 minutes

Reuse a round completed in Lesson 3, or copy the packaged preview as a rehearsal starting point, and state its source.
Give this round's result.md to wake_review.py, set to fire once after five seconds with a maximum call time of ninety seconds, writing to a new one-shot-review/.
Together check the host and time zone in run.json, the planned and actual times, the input hash, the exit code and the final review.md.
Only a completed run hands its review comments to a new session; for a timeout, failure or empty output, record the reason first and do not advance the review to completed.
The new session reads only LOOP.md, STATE.md and this round's records, checks them, then takes over and finishes the round, generating the local page and checking for duplicated effects.

The instructor and students check the command together before running it. Replace the path with this round’s actual record, and use a new output directory each time:

python3 wake_review.py --input preview/runs/1/result.md --out one-shot-review --delay-seconds 5 --timeout-seconds 90

Pressing Ctrl+C during the planned wait and then confirming that the status is cancelled and calls_started is zero verifies that no call was made. Pressing Ctrl+C during the run stops this subprocess. Finally, confirm that the process has exited and that no periodic wake-up remains.

The task lock determines which session may write; the run ID identifies the action being recorded. A lock timeout requires a check of the holder and its process; if the old session can still resume writing, deleting the lock on the basis of time alone creates two writers.

Exercise 3: recording a candidate playbook entry and its test, 10 minutes

Distill at most one playbook entry from this round's failure or success, writing its conditions of use, the concrete action and its source.
List which kind of unseen task it is expected to change, and the main evaluation metric.
Keep a no-playbook control, then design a placebo control in the same format and of similar length.
Mark the entry as a candidate first and put the test plan into the next batch of tasks; do not take this round's success as proof that it works.

Once the current loop is complete, a small number of timed runs are configured after class. The stop conditions, the recovery method and the response to exhausted quota are confirmed before the unattended period is extended.

Common errors and summary, 5 minutes

Common errors and corrections

Restarting from the first step at every wake-up. The state refers to products and action identities, and recovery first checks which effects have already occurred.

Assuming that a lock rules out concurrency. Check who holds the lock and whether the old session can still write after a takeover; all shared writes go through the same entry point.

Concluding that the system has improved because the number of rounds has increased. Fix the unseen tasks and the scoring standard, and compare whether a repeatable gain appears.

Discarding failed runs so that the results page shows only successes. Only when failed rounds retain their status and cause is it possible to judge which stage an improvement affects.

Method summary

The files from earlier sessions are now connected by state transitions. The one-shot timer starts the session, the runner records run details, the reviewer assesses the conclusions, and the playbook proposes methods for future tasks. The results page brings these together in one place, from which the next step can be decided.

AI Scientist assigns similar responsibilities to separate modules. In this course, the minimal loop is first established with a single research question before the workload is scaled up.

Review and extension

Conceptual review, analysis of contrasting examples, and the independent and transfer tasks specified in Assessment are required for this session. Other extension topics are optional, except for the required readings identified below.

Unaided review, 15 minutes

Reading only the class STATE.md and this round’s files, list what the next person to take over should query first. Then, without the agent, trace failure recovery through the state diagram, separating a failed program execution from a negative scientific result. Record in review-after-class.md any transitions that still rely on guesswork, and add completion conditions that can be checked.

Analysis of contrasting examples, 20 minutes

Procedure A sees that the status is still “not started” and immediately resends the experiment. Procedure B first queries with the recorded job identifier, finds that the product has already been generated, and only completes the status. State the possible effect of each within the disconnection window. Then consider an experiment that ran normally but refuted its prediction: should the program be retried, or should the round proceed to the conclusion and a candidate playbook entry? Give reasons.

Draw the recovery path before reading the commentary

When the status lags behind the actual action, A may execute the action twice. B checks the effect through the action identity, so its recovery has a basis. A negative scientific result from a program that succeeded with complete products can be archived normally; whether a playbook entry is effective is still left to testing on unseen tasks.

Further study

First repeat interruption recovery once, choosing a failure point different from the one used in class, then choose one path that builds on the single wake-up from class. Periodic scheduling begins with a small number of runs, and the controls for pausing and deleting the schedule are checked; external notification and deployment are limited to targets and authorizations already established.

When the exercise is repeated with a student’s own topic, choose a step that can be interrupted safely. The recovery rules should survive one actual failure before the unattended period is extended.

Assessment

Attainment of the outcomes is judged from the student’s explanation of the loop’s behavior, not from a single successful run. Students should be able to state the completion condition of each transition; explain, for a chosen failure point, which record allows recovery to determine whether the action took effect; show that a second failure point, different from the one demonstrated in class, is recovered without duplicated effects; and justify why one round’s success does not establish the value of a playbook entry. Transfer is assessed by applying the same state model to another step or to the student’s own workflow and identifying where its recovery rules would need to change.

Independent work must also complete the target-playbook, matched-placebo and no-playbook comparison on unseen tasks under matched budgets. Students must report the group results, effect comparisons and uncertainty, and explain the conditions to which the findings apply. Unfinished tests remain pending; evaluation of an entry rests on the observed comparison.
