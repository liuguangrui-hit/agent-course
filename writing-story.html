1.1 论文表达

课后深入，约 45 分钟。

同一份数据既可以按实验逐项报告，也可以围绕一个发现展开。动笔之前，先确定希望读者记住什么。

选择核心发现

练习里可以比较 GCE 与交叉熵的最终准确率，也可以从训练轨迹开篇。前一种开法马上进入方法排名；后一种先让读者看到网络早期表现好、后期退步，再追问怎样保住已经学到的答案。

选择一个开头，同时给后文分配职责。解释时间变化的曲线先出场，比较噪声水平的图随后出场，方法细节放在读者需要它的时候。暂时放不进主线的材料，先移到提纲之外，判断它是否回答了一个独立问题。

读完整稿件和结果。给三个开篇候选，每个说明读者最自然的下一问，以及哪张图能回答。
挑其中一条，按问题与回答写全文提纲。每段只写一句结论和一个证据入口。

标题与摘要

标题要让大同行知道研究改变了什么认识。比较三个候选：一个说研究对象，一个说方法，一个直接说发现。读给同方向的同学听，看看哪一个能让对方提出与论文相关的问题。

摘要可以沿五步展开：先给核心现象，接着点出解释，再说怎样检验，给关键结果，最后落到这项发现改变的选择。不同论文可以调整这五步；每一步都要有具体内容。

以练习为例，开篇可以直接写网络很早找到正确答案，继续训练却退步。随后用训练曲线给读者一个具体画面，再引入损失函数怎样分配学习力度。把普遍背景压到理解这个现象所需的程度。

结果节按读者的疑问排

让每节开头先给这一节的回答，然后给证据。一个适合练习的顺序是：

  1. 展示先上升后下降的训练轨迹，确认现象。
  2. 比较不同损失留下的最终表现，提出解释。
  3. 改变噪声比例，检查解释能否继续组织结果。
  4. 讨论早停与样本选择怎样进一步区分解释。

第 4 步自然通向后续课程。文章的推进来自尚未被回答的问题，结尾因此能指出下一项有价值的实验。

范文的段落功能

读范文时，在每段旁边写它做了什么：建立矛盾，提出核心想法，排除另一种解释，或交代实现。随后用自己的材料完成相应的叙事功能。

为 exemplar/ 中的范文做一张段落作用表，概括作用，不摘抄句子。
再看自己的提纲：哪些段落反复承担同一个作用，哪些关键追问还没有回答？
重排提纲后，另起一稿写开篇和第一个结果小节。

重讲后的完整性核查

把原稿的数据事实做成一页记录，另列用户此前纠正过的问题。重讲后逐项核对：数字的对象和分母是否一样，方法是否仍可复现，读者是否能从主张走到对应证据。

把标题和核心主张在前后版本中并排列出，检查是否还在回答同一个问题。审阅意见怎样处理，见 1.4 审稿返修。

练习

给同一个发现写三种开头：从结果开始，从一个例子开始，从竞争解释开始。请一位没有参加写作的读者读，然后只问对方下一步想知道什么。

怎样判断

如果对方追问的是论文接下来能回答的问题，这个开头已经建立推进。若对方先问某个缩写是什么意思，补足必要背景；若对方只说做了很多工作，核心发现还不够清楚。

常见错误与处理方法

提纲只有话题，没有回答。 把方法比较改成一句能被数据检验的结论，再决定用哪项证据展开。

范文变成句式模仿。 要求它交段落作用表，再用自己的发现重新写。让读者理解并记住研究内容。

每轮返修只看最近的意见。 把历次用户纠正汇成一页，重讲前重读，完稿后逐项查回归。

继续读

Simon Peyton Jones 的写作讲座适合对照提纲；Gopen 与 Swan 的 The Science of Scientific Writing可用来检查句子给读者的期待。

1.1 Structuring a paper

After-class deep dive, about 45 minutes.

You can present the same data as a list of experiments or build a paper around a finding readers want to understand. Before writing, decide what you want readers to remember.

Choose a finding to lead with

In the exercise you could compare the final accuracy of GCE and cross-entropy, or you could open with the training trajectory. The first opening goes straight into a ranking of methods; the second shows the reader that the network does well early and gets worse later, then asks how to keep the answers it has already learned.

Choosing an opening also assigns jobs to what follows. The curve that explains change over time comes first, the figure comparing noise levels follows, and method details appear when the reader needs them. Material that does not yet fit the main argument moves outside the outline for now, while you judge whether it answers a separate question.

Read the whole draft and the results. Give three candidate openings, and for each say what the reader would most naturally ask next and which figure answers it.
Choose one and write an outline of the full paper as questions and answers. For each paragraph, write one sentence stating its conclusion and identify the supporting evidence.

Writing the title and abstract

The title should tell readers in neighboring fields how the research changes our understanding. Compare three candidates: one names the object of study, one names the method, one states the finding directly. Read them to a classmate in the same area and see which one leads them to ask a question the paper deals with.

An abstract can unfold in five steps: give the core phenomenon, point to the explanation, say how it was tested, give the key result, and end on the decision this finding informs. Different papers can adjust the five steps; each step needs specific content.

In the exercise, the opening can say directly that the network finds the right answers early, yet gets worse with further training. The training curve then gives the reader a concrete picture, followed by how the loss function distributes learning effort. Cut general background down to what is needed to understand the phenomenon.

Order the results by the reader’s questions

Start each section with that section’s answer, then give the evidence. One order that suits the exercise:

  1. Show the training trajectory that rises and then falls, confirming the phenomenon.
  2. Compare the final performance achieved with different losses, and propose an explanation.
  3. Change the noise rate and check whether the explanation still accounts for the results.
  4. Discuss how early stopping and sample selection could separate explanations further.

Step 4 leads naturally into later lessons. A paper moves forward through questions not yet answered, so its ending can point to the next experiment worth doing.

Analyze each paragraph’s role

When reading the exemplar, write beside each paragraph what it does: sets up a tension, proposes the core idea, rules out another explanation, or explains the implementation. Then use your own material to do the same narrative job.

Make a table of paragraph functions for the exemplar in exemplar/, summarizing each job without copying sentences.
Then look at my outline: which paragraphs repeat the same job, and which key follow-up questions are still unanswered?
After reordering the outline, write the opening and the first results subsection as a new draft.

Check completeness after restructuring

Put the factual results in the original draft on one page, and list separately the problems the user has corrected before. After restructuring, check each item: is each number about the same object with the same denominator, can the method still be reproduced, can the reader get from each claim to its evidence?

List the title and core claims of the old and new versions side by side and check that they still answer the same question. For how to handle review comments, see 1.4 Peer review and revision.

Exercise

Write three openings for the same finding: one starting from the result, one from an example, one from a competing explanation. Have a reader who did not take part in the writing read them, then ask them only what they want to know next.

How to judge

If they ask about something the paper goes on to answer, the opening gives readers a reason to continue. If they first ask what an abbreviation means, add the necessary background; if they only say a lot of work was done, the core finding is still unclear.

Common mistakes and how to fix them

The outline has only topics, no answers. Turn the method comparison into one conclusion the data can test, then decide which evidence develops it.

The exemplar turns into imitation of sentence patterns. Ask for a table of paragraph functions, then rewrite with your own finding. Readers should remember the research; the prose should guide them through it.

Each round of revision looks only at the latest comments. Collect all the user’s past corrections on one page, reread it before restructuring, and check each for regressions after finishing.

Further reading

Simon Peyton Jones’s talk on writing is good to compare against an outline; Gopen and Swan’s The Science of Scientific Writing helps check what each sentence leads the reader to expect.
