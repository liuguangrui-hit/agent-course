1.1 论文表达

学习目标

  1. 比较同一结果的不同开篇，选择能引出研究问题的核心发现，并据此为后文各部分分配论证职责。
  2. 分析范文段落的叙事功能，把这些功能迁移到自己的材料上，而不模仿其句式。
  3. 对照重写前后的数据事实、标题与核心主张，检查重写是否保持了完整性。

课后深入专题，约 45 分钟。

同一份数据既可以按实验逐项报告，也可以围绕一个发现展开论证。动笔之前，应先确定希望读者记住什么。

核心发现的选择

以练习材料为例，可以从 GCE 与交叉熵的最终准确率比较开篇，也可以从训练轨迹开篇。前一种写法直接进入方法排名；后一种先呈现网络早期表现良好、后期退步，再追问怎样保住已经学到的内容。

选定开篇，也就为后文分配了职责。解释时间变化的曲线先出现，比较噪声水平的图随后出现，方法细节在读者需要时再交代。暂时放不进主线的材料先移出提纲，再判断它是否回答了一个独立的问题。

读完整稿件和结果。给三个开篇候选，每个说明读者最自然的下一问，以及哪张图能回答。
挑其中一条，按问题与回答写全文提纲。每段只写一句结论和一个证据入口。

标题与摘要

标题应让相关领域的读者知道研究改变了哪项认识。可以比较三个候选：一个陈述研究对象，一个陈述方法，一个直接陈述发现。读给同方向的同学听，观察哪一个能让对方提出与论文内容相关的问题。

摘要可按五个步骤展开：核心现象，解释，检验方式，关键结果，以及这项发现所影响的研究选择。不同论文可以调整这五步，但每一步都要有具体内容。

以练习论文为例，开篇可以直接陈述网络很早就学到正确答案，继续训练却出现退步。随后用训练曲线给出具体证据，再引入损失函数怎样分配学习力度。一般性背景只保留到理解这一现象所必需的程度。

按读者疑问安排结果节

每节开头先给出本节的结论，再给出证据。练习论文可采用如下顺序：

  1. 展示先上升后下降的训练轨迹，确认现象。
  2. 比较不同损失的最终表现，提出解释。
  3. 改变噪声比例，检验该解释能否继续说明结果。
  4. 讨论早停与样本选择怎样进一步区分不同解释。

第 4 步自然衔接后续课程。论文的推进依靠尚未回答的问题，结尾因此可以指出下一项有价值的实验。

范文的段落功能分析

阅读范文时，在每段旁注明它承担的功能：建立矛盾，提出核心想法，排除另一种解释，或交代实现细节。随后用自己的材料完成相应的叙事功能。

为 exemplar/ 中的范文做一张段落作用表，概括作用，不摘抄句子。
再看自己的提纲：哪些段落反复承担同一个作用，哪些关键追问还没有回答？
重排提纲后，另起一稿写开篇和第一个结果小节。

重写后的完整性核查

把原稿的数据事实整理为一页记录，另列此前用户已纠正过的问题。重写后逐项核对：数字的对象和分母是否一致，方法是否仍可复现，读者能否从每项主张找到对应证据。

把前后版本的标题和核心主张并排列出，检查它们是否仍在回答同一个问题。审阅意见的处理见 1.4 审稿返修。

练习

为同一个发现写三种开头：从结果开始，从一个例子开始，从竞争解释开始。请一位未参与写作的读者阅读，只询问对方接下来想知道什么。

判断标准

如果读者追问的是论文后文能够回答的问题，这个开头已经建立了论证推进。若读者先问某个缩写的含义，应补足必要背景；若读者只感到做了很多工作，说明核心发现仍不够清楚。

常见错误与处理方法

提纲只列话题，没有结论。 把方法比较改写成一句可由数据检验的结论，再决定用哪项证据展开。

把范文分析变成句式模仿。 要求 Agent 提交段落作用表，再用自己的发现重新写作，使读者理解并记住研究内容。

每轮返修只看最近一轮意见。 把历次用户纠正汇总为一页，重写前重读，完稿后逐项检查是否出现回退。

延伸阅读

Simon Peyton Jones 的写作讲座可用于对照提纲；Gopen 与 Swan 的 The Science of Scientific Writing可用于检查句子结构给读者造成的预期。

1.1 Structuring a paper

Learning outcomes

After this section, students should be able to:

  1. Compare candidate openings for the same results by the questions each leads a reader to ask, and select a central finding on that basis.
  2. Analyze the argumentative function of each paragraph in an exemplar and reproduce that structure with different material.
  3. Check that a restructured paper preserves every factual result and the scope of its title and core claims.

Extension reading and exercises, about 45 minutes.

The same data can be presented as a list of experiments or as an argument built around a finding that readers want to understand. The decision about what readers should remember precedes the writing.

Selecting the leading finding

In the exercise paper, the opening could compare the final accuracy of GCE and cross-entropy, or it could begin with the training trajectory. The first option leads directly into a ranking of methods. The second shows that the network performs well early and deteriorates later, and then asks how the correct answers learned early can be retained.

The choice of opening also assigns roles to what follows. The curve that explains change over time comes first, the figure comparing noise levels follows, and method details appear where the reader needs them. Material that does not yet serve the main argument is set aside while the author judges whether it answers a separate question.

Read the whole draft and the results. Give three candidate openings, and for each say what the reader would most naturally ask next and which figure answers it.
Choose one and write an outline of the full paper as questions and answers. For each paragraph, write one sentence stating its conclusion and identify the supporting evidence.

Writing the title and abstract

The title should tell readers in neighboring fields how the research changes their understanding. Compare three candidates: one naming the object of study, one naming the method and one stating the finding directly. A useful test is to read them to a colleague in the same area and observe which one prompts a question the paper actually addresses.

An abstract can proceed in five steps: state the core phenomenon, indicate the explanation, describe how it was tested, give the key result, and close with the decision the finding informs. The steps can be adapted to different papers, but each must contain specific content.

In the exercise, the opening can state directly that the network finds the correct answers early and deteriorates with further training. The training curve then gives the reader a concrete picture, followed by an account of how the loss function distributes learning effort. General background is reduced to what is needed to understand the phenomenon.

Ordering results by the reader’s questions

Each section begins with its answer and then gives the evidence. One order suited to the exercise is:

  1. Show the training trajectory that rises and then falls, establishing the phenomenon.
  2. Compare the final performance obtained with different losses, and propose an explanation.
  3. Vary the noise rate and check whether the explanation still accounts for the results.
  4. Discuss how early stopping and sample selection could further separate the explanations.

Step 4 leads into later sessions of the course. A paper advances through questions not yet answered, so its conclusion can identify the next experiment worth doing.

Analyzing paragraph functions

When reading the exemplar, note beside each paragraph the function it serves: establishing a tension, proposing the core idea, ruling out an alternative explanation, or explaining the implementation. The same functions are then carried out with the student’s own material.

Make a table of paragraph functions for the exemplar in exemplar/, summarizing each job without copying sentences.
Then look at my outline: which paragraphs repeat the same job, and which key follow-up questions are still unanswered?
After reordering the outline, write the opening and the first results subsection as a new draft.

Checking completeness after restructuring

Collect the factual results of the original draft on one page, and list separately the problems the user has corrected previously. After restructuring, check each item: whether each number still refers to the same object with the same denominator, whether the method can still be reproduced, and whether a reader can get from each claim to its evidence.

Place the title and core claims of the old and new versions side by side and confirm that they still answer the same question. The handling of review comments is covered in 1.4 Peer review and revision.

Exercise

Write three openings for the same finding: one starting from the result, one from an example and one from a competing explanation. Give them to a reader who was not involved in the writing, and ask only what they would want to know next.

Assessment criteria

If the reader asks about something the paper goes on to answer, the opening gives a reason to continue. If the reader first asks what an abbreviation means, the necessary background is missing; if the reader says only that a great deal of work was done, the core finding is still unclear.

Common errors and corrections

An outline of topics without answers. Turn the method comparison into one conclusion the data can test, then decide which evidence develops it.

Imitating the exemplar’s sentence patterns. Ask for a table of paragraph functions, then rewrite with the paper’s own finding. Readers should remember the research; the prose should guide them through it.

Addressing only the latest comments in each revision. Collect all of the user’s past corrections on one page, reread it before restructuring and check each item for regressions afterwards.

Further reading

Simon Peyton Jones’s talk on writing is useful to compare against an outline; Gopen and Swan’s The Science of Scientific Writing helps to check what each sentence leads the reader to expect.
