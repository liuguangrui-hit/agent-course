1.1 论文的讲法

课后深入，约 45 分钟。用到第 1 课练习材料。

审稿人一天要看好几篇论文，每篇先给题目和摘要几十秒。这几十秒决定他是带着兴趣读下去，还是带着挑剔读下去。这一节讲怎样把一篇论文讲成让人想读下去的样子，以及怎样让 Agent 照这个讲法写。

先找到那一个点

一篇论文里通常有好几个结果，能拿来开篇的只有一个。好的开篇结果有三个特征：出人意料，带数字，具体到能在脑子里成像。

练习论文里有四个候选：

  1. 在 40% 噪声下，GCE 的最终准确率比交叉熵高 28.6 个点。
  2. 噪声越大，GCE 的优势越大，从 15.7 个点到 36.6 个点。
  3. 交叉熵第 3 轮就学到 92%，之后 77 轮都在记错误标签，最后掉到 67%。
  4. 标签平滑没有任何保护作用。

第 1 条和第 2 条是比较，读者要先知道 GCE 是什么才会在意。第 4 条是否定，适合放在后面回答追问。第 3 条是一个故事：网络明明已经学会了，又亲手忘掉。它不需要任何背景就能听懂，而且马上引出下一个问题，那怎么才能不忘。这就是开篇的那个点。

让 Agent 帮你找时，不要问最重要的结果是什么，它会给你最全面的那个。换一个问法：

读 paper/main.tex 和 data/。列出这篇论文里所有能拿来开篇的结果，每个写一句话，带数字。
然后按这个标准排序：审稿人读完会不会转头讲给同事听。说明排第一的理由。

题目

题目是招牌，不是摘要的缩写。Attention Is All You Need 没告诉你做了什么，但没人记不住它。Understanding deep learning requires rethinking generalization 是一句论断，读完就知道作者要推翻什么。

初稿的题目是 Towards an Empirical Understanding of the Robustness of Loss Functions to Label Noise: A Preliminary Study on Two-Layer Neural Networks。二十多个词里，Towards，Empirical，Preliminary 三个词都在提前道歉，Two-Layer Neural Networks 把适用范围也写进了题目。

重讲后的题目是 Bounded Losses Do Not Memorize Wrong Labels。主语是方法，谓语是那个现象，读者能立刻判断它对不对。

几条规则：

  • 尽量写成一句论断，主谓俱全。
  • 十个词以内，冒号后的副题能省就省。
  • 用领域通行的叫法，不在题目里放自造的名字。
  • 防御类工作可以用盾牌，防火墙一类的形象说法，让人一眼看出是防御。
  • 不要让 Agent 一次只给一个题目。让它给三个，你来选，再让它沿着你选的方向改。

摘要

一个好用的五句结构：

  1. 那个点，带数字。
  2. 为什么会这样，一句话讲机制。
  3. 我们做了什么，规模多大。
  4. 主要发现，每个只带一个数字。
  5. 落点，读者该带走什么。

初稿摘要的开头：

In recent years, deep neural networks have achieved remarkable success across a wide range of tasks. However, it is worth noting that real-world datasets inevitably contain noisy labels, which may, to some extent, adversely affect model performance.

两句话没有一个信息是审稿人不知道的。重讲后的开头：

Flip 40% of the training labels and a two-layer network trained with cross-entropy reaches 92% test accuracy by epoch 3, then spends the next 77 epochs memorizing the wrong labels and ends at 67%.

第一句就是那个故事。局限不进摘要，摘要里每一句都在往前推。

引言

引言的第一段讲那个例子，讲得比摘要更具体：哪张图，什么条件，数字是多少。第二段才给背景，而且只给理解这个例子所需的背景。第三段讲机制或方法的核心想法。最后一段按读者会追问的顺序，把论文要回答的问题说出来，指向对应章节。

初稿的引言末尾是一个四条的贡献清单，把摘要又说了一遍，最后一条是 We discuss the limitations of our study。这种清单在很多领域是惯例，但如果要用，每条只带一个数字，不能和摘要重复，更不要把讨论局限算成贡献。

结果

初稿按实验编号组织：RQ1，RQ2，RQ3。这是作者做实验的顺序，不是读者想知道的顺序。

读者读完引言，脑子里的问题是：这个现象是真的吗；换个噪声比例还成立吗；常用的替代办法行不行。结果节就按这个顺序排，每一段的第一句直接是结论：

  • Cross-entropy learns the task, then unlearns it.
  • The bounded loss leads at every noise rate.
  • Label smoothing gives no protection.
  • Dropping high-loss samples works when the noise rate is known.

只读这四句，就知道整个结果节说了什么。

讨论与局限

局限只讲一次，放在讨论里，写成证据范围的陈述：这些结果来自什么样的设置，哪些条件没有覆盖。初稿把合成数据这条限定从摘要一路说到结论，共说了四遍，还在两个结果段后面各加了一句 It should be noted that。讲一次，读者就记住了；讲四次，读者会以为作者自己都不信。

不要写 Our conclusions should be interpreted with caution 这种话。它没有提供信息，只表达了不安。

结论

一到两句。不要把摘要再说一遍，也不要写 We hope this work can provide insights。重讲后的结论只有两句：交叉熵把最大的梯度给了最可能错的标签，有界损失把这个梯度压下去，网络就留住了前几轮找到的答案。

范文怎么用

范文从目标刊会里挑，挑你自己读着最舒服的那篇。让 Agent 学的是结构，不是措辞：

读 exemplar/ 里的论文。不要摘抄句子。
告诉我它的题目，摘要的每一句和引言的每一段各承担什么作用，写成一张表。
然后按这张表的结构，为 paper/main.tex 写一个提纲，每一行写这一段要讲的结论。先停下来等我看提纲。

先看提纲再让它写全文，比写完再改省事得多。提纲阶段改一行，全文阶段要改一段。

练习

用你自己正在写的一篇论文，让 Agent 列出所有能开篇的结果并排序，然后为排第一的结果写三个版本的引言第一段：一个从结果开始，一个从一个具体例子开始，一个从一个反常识的问题开始。读三个版本，选一个。

练习论文的参考写法

从结果开始：A network trained on noisy labels often knows the right answer early and forgets it later.

从例子开始：In our two-layer network with 40% of labels flipped, cross-entropy reaches 92% test accuracy at epoch 3 and falls to 67% by epoch 80.

从问题开始：Why does a network that has already found the right answer spend 77 more epochs unlearning it?

重讲稿用了第一种接第二种：先一句概括，紧接着用例子落地。

最容易出错的地方

题目追求准确。 Agent 起题目时会把方法，设置和范围全写进去，越改越长。告诉它题目是招牌，给它看几个名作的题目，要求写成一句论断。

开篇先铺背景。 不加约束时，Agent 的第一句几乎总是 In recent years。在任务卡里明确第一句写什么，比事后删掉有效。

先否定再收窄。 We do not claim X. Rather, we show the narrower Y. 这种句子读起来像在辩护。直接写 Y，X 为什么不成立放进讨论。

段首只写话题。 Memorization. Robust losses. 这样的段首标题只告诉读者这段讲什么，没告诉他结论。改成一句结论，读者扫一遍段首就能读懂整节。

用自造的名字。 Agent 喜欢给方法起名字，然后全文都用这个名字。大同行没见过这个词，每读到一次都要回想一次。用领域里通行的叫法，必须起名时只在一处定义。

延伸阅读

  • Simon Peyton Jones, How to write a great research paper：把论文当成讲一个想法，先想清楚要讲哪一个。
  • George Gopen and Judith Swan, The Science of Scientific Writing：读者期待信息出现在句子的什么位置。
  • George Whitesides, Whitesides’ Group: Writing a Paper：从提纲开始写论文。

1.1 How a paper is told

Self-study, about 45 minutes. Uses the Session 1 materials.

A reviewer reads several papers a day and gives each title and abstract a few dozen seconds first. Those seconds decide whether the reviewer reads on with interest or with a red pen. This section is about telling a paper so that people want to keep reading, and about getting an agent to write it that way.

First find the one point

A paper usually has several results, and only one of them can open it. A good opening result has three traits: it is surprising, it has a number, and it is concrete enough to picture.

The exercise paper has four candidates:

  1. At 40% noise, GCE’s final accuracy is 28.6 points higher than cross-entropy’s.
  2. The more noise, the bigger GCE’s advantage, from 15.7 points to 36.6 points.
  3. Cross-entropy reaches 92% by epoch 3, spends the next 77 epochs memorizing the wrong labels, and ends at 67%.
  4. Label smoothing gives no protection at all.

Candidates 1 and 2 are comparisons; a reader has to know what GCE is before caring. Candidate 4 is a negative, better kept for answering follow-up questions later. Candidate 3 is a story: the network has learned the task, then unlearns it with its own hands. Anyone can follow it with no background, and it raises the next question at once: how do you stop it forgetting? That is the point to open with.

When you ask an agent to find it, don’t ask for the most important result. You will get the most comprehensive one. Ask this way instead:

Read paper/main.tex and data/. List every result in this paper that could open it, one sentence each, with its number.
Then rank them by this test: after reading it, would a reviewer turn around and tell a colleague? Explain why the top one ranks first.

Title

A title is a sign meant to be remembered. The abstract does the summarizing. Attention Is All You Need does not tell you what the paper did, yet nobody forgets it. Understanding deep learning requires rethinking generalization is a claim, and once you have read it you know what the authors set out to overturn.

The draft’s title is Towards an Empirical Understanding of the Robustness of Loss Functions to Label Noise: A Preliminary Study on Two-Layer Neural Networks. In twenty-odd words, Towards, Empirical and Preliminary all apologize in advance, and Two-Layer Neural Networks writes the scope into the title.

The retold title is Bounded Losses Do Not Memorize Wrong Labels. The subject is the method, the verb phrase is the phenomenon, and a reader can judge at once whether it is true.

A few rules:

  • Make it a claim whenever you can, with a subject and a verb.
  • Ten words or fewer; drop the subtitle after the colon if you can.
  • Use the field’s standard terms, never a name you coined.
  • Defensive work can use images such as a shield or a firewall, so readers see at a glance that it is about defense.
  • Don’t let the agent give you one title at a time. Have it give three, choose one yourself, then have it refine along the direction you chose.

Abstract

A five-sentence structure that works:

  1. The point, with its number.
  2. Why it happens, the mechanism in one sentence.
  3. What we did, and at what scale.
  4. The main findings, one number each.
  5. The landing: what the reader should take away.

The draft abstract opens:

In recent years, deep neural networks have achieved remarkable success across a wide range of tasks. However, it is worth noting that real-world datasets inevitably contain noisy labels, which may, to some extent, adversely affect model performance.

Those two sentences tell a reviewer nothing new. The retold opening:

Flip 40% of the training labels and a two-layer network trained with cross-entropy reaches 92% test accuracy by epoch 3, then spends the next 77 epochs memorizing the wrong labels and ends at 67%.

The first sentence is the story. Limitations stay out of the abstract, and every sentence in it moves forward.

Introduction

The first paragraph of the introduction tells the example, in more detail than the abstract: which figure, under what conditions, with what numbers. Background comes in the second paragraph, and only the background needed to understand the example. The third paragraph gives the core idea of the mechanism or method. The last paragraph states the questions the paper answers, in the order a reader would ask them, each pointing to its section.

The draft’s introduction ends with a four-item list of contributions that repeats the abstract, and the last item is We discuss the limitations of our study. Many fields expect such a list. If you use one, give each item one number, don’t repeat the abstract, and never count a discussion of limitations as a contribution.

Results

The draft organizes results by experiment number: RQ1, RQ2, RQ3. That is the order the authors ran the experiments in. Readers want something else.

After the introduction, the reader’s questions are: is this phenomenon real; does it still hold at other noise rates; do the usual alternatives work? The results section follows that order, and the first sentence of each paragraph is the conclusion itself:

  • Cross-entropy learns the task, then unlearns it.
  • The bounded loss leads at every noise rate.
  • Label smoothing gives no protection.
  • Dropping high-loss samples works when the noise rate is known.

Read only those four sentences and you know what the whole results section says.

Discussion and limitations

State the limitations once, in the discussion, as a statement of the evidence’s scope: what kind of setting the results come from, and which conditions they do not cover. The draft carries the synthetic-data caveat from the abstract all the way to the conclusion, four times in all, and adds an It should be noted that after two of the results paragraphs as well. Say it once and the reader remembers it. Say it four times and the reader concludes that the authors don’t believe their own results.

Don’t write sentences like Our conclusions should be interpreted with caution. They carry no information, only unease.

Conclusion

One or two sentences. Don’t restate the abstract, and don’t write We hope this work can provide insights. The retold conclusion has just two sentences: Cross-entropy gives its largest gradient to the labels most likely to be wrong. A bounded loss turns that gradient down, and the network keeps the answer it found in the first few epochs.

How to use an exemplar

Pick the exemplar from the target venue, the paper you yourself find most pleasant to read. The agent should learn its structure and leave its wording alone:

Read the paper in exemplar/. Don't copy any sentences.
Tell me the job its title does, and the job of each sentence of its abstract and each paragraph of its introduction, as a table.
Then follow that table's structure to write an outline for paper/main.tex, one line per paragraph stating the conclusion that paragraph makes. Stop and wait for me to review the outline.

Reviewing the outline before the agent writes the full paper is far less work than fixing the paper afterwards. A one-line change at the outline stage is a one-paragraph change in the full text.

Exercise

Take a paper you are writing. Have the agent list every result that could open it and rank them, then write three versions of the introduction’s first paragraph for the top result: one that opens with the result, one that opens with a concrete example, and one that opens with a counterintuitive question. Read all three and choose one.

Sample answers for the exercise paper

Opening with the result: A network trained on noisy labels often knows the right answer early and forgets it later.

Opening with an example: In our two-layer network with 40% of labels flipped, cross-entropy reaches 92% test accuracy at epoch 3 and falls to 67% by epoch 80.

Opening with a question: Why does a network that has already found the right answer spend 77 more epochs unlearning it?

The retold paper uses the first followed by the second: one sentence of summary, then the example to ground it.

Where agents go wrong most

Titles that chase accuracy. When an agent writes a title it packs in the method, the setting and the scope, and each revision makes it longer. Tell it a title is a sign, show it a few famous titles, and ask for a claim.

Opening with background. Left unconstrained, an agent’s first sentence is almost always In recent years. Saying in the task card what the first sentence should be works better than deleting it afterwards.

Denying first, then narrowing. We do not claim X. Rather, we show the narrower Y. Sentences like this read like a defense. Write Y directly, and put why X fails in the discussion.

Paragraph leads that only name a topic. Leads like Memorization. Robust losses. tell readers what the paragraph is about and leave out its conclusion. Make each lead a conclusion, and a reader who skims the leads understands the whole section.

Coined names. Agents like to name a method and then use the name throughout. Readers from neighboring fields have never seen the word and have to recall it every time it appears. Use the field’s standard terms, and if you must coin a name, define it in one place only.

Further reading

  • Simon Peyton Jones, How to write a great research paper: treat a paper as conveying one idea, and decide which one before you write.
  • George Gopen and Judith Swan, The Science of Scientific Writing: where in a sentence readers expect each piece of information.
  • George Whitesides, Whitesides’ Group: Writing a Paper: writing a paper starting from the outline.
