课程大纲

课程主线

课程采用带噪标签学习实验作为贯穿案例。必修部分依次开展论文表达与结果核查、文献证据分析、判别实验设计，以及可恢复科研流程的构建。统一案例的规模经过控制，以支持课堂内完成数据核查、错误分析和方法比较。

跨学科应用可结合材料筛选、计算生物学或社会科学中的具体问题，调整数据类型与验证条件。迁移练习须明确原始材料、研究判断与实验检验之间的关系，并说明证据更新对后续研究环节的影响。

教学组织

课前准备

首次上课前须完成第 0 课的环境准备。后续各课依据准备要求安装新增工具、下载材料与依赖、完成账号登录，并检查环境可用性。耗时较长的安装与下载建议提前一天完成；复杂配置与研究判断在课堂中集中处理。

准备检查记录须包含系统与软件版本、材料位置、已通过的检查及未解决问题。环境问题应在开课前反馈给授课教师，必要时使用备用材料参与课堂。下载、登录等操作性问题安排在课堂之外处理。

课堂教学

每次课堂教学为 60 分钟，由准备检查、概念讲授、实践分析和成果核查构成。课堂实践使用预先取得的材料及短时运行，及时检验输出。最后五分钟用于归纳常见错误、整理实践成果及确定后续任务。

各课分别处理配置合并、证据可比性、判别实验设计、状态恢复与学习效应识别等问题。完整训练、批量文献获取和长期调度安排在课后执行，其研究计划、执行条件与完成判据在课堂中明确。

课后复习与扩展学习

课后先安排约 15 分钟独立复述关键概念，再依据课堂记录核查理解。随后用 15 至 20 分钟分析正反例，提交判断及理由后参照解析。扩展学习围绕同一研究问题展开，要求将课程方法应用于本人研究材料。

复习与扩展学习分别形成学习记录。扩展作业可采用推导、实验或系统配置等形式，须保留实际过程与结果。各课提供多条选做路径，可按研究需要分次完成。

各所修单元的复习、独立实践与迁移评价为必做要求；深入专题中指定的必读片段须完成，其余内容按需选做。

各课页面设有本节课目标，并在课后部分给出能力达成评价。评价分三个层次：课堂训练检查方法的初步运用，课后独立完成检查学生能否解释方法并给出判断依据，迁移检查在更换材料或条件后方法是否仍能正确使用。

必修部分的结课成果包括一轮完整研究记录及一次中断恢复演示。评价重点为证据可追溯性、实验设计与结果解释，以及流程恢复的正确性。选修部分分别提交安全测试记录或学习收益评估记录与方案。执行状态与科学结论分别核查，研究判断须由对应证据支持。

先修条件与环境要求

学习者应具备基本的科研文献阅读能力，了解所在领域常用的数据分析方法；无需已有 Agent 开发经验。准备一台 Windows、macOS 或 Linux 电脑，并按第 0 课完成安装与首次文件读写。写作与审阅原则上使用不同厂商的 Agent；仅具备单一厂商条件时，可使用独立新会话完成练习并注明执行者。

第 2 课须预先取得课堂使用的开放全文；涉及订阅材料时，提前完成机构登录。第 3 课可在本机完成，GitHub 与服务器按已有条件选用。第 4 课不以真实通知和部署为先修条件。课程不要求购买 API 额度或新增云服务器，系统授权和账号登录由学习者本人完成。

课程参考

课程结构参考 UChicago AI Agents for Science 的科研项目实践，以及 Carpentries 的分节设计。进阶内容对照 Berkeley LLM Agents、Hugging Face Agents Course 和 DeepLearning.AI Evaluating AI Agents。MIT Missing Semester 的 Agentic Coding可对照看工具怎样进入真实工作。工具操作参考 Anthropic 官方课程 与 OpenAI Codex CLI 教程。

Syllabus

Course progression

A shared case study on learning with noisy labels runs through the course. The required sessions progress from research communication and verification to literature analysis, discriminating experiments, and recoverable research workflows. The case is kept small enough to support data verification, error analysis, and method comparison during class.

Applications in materials screening, computational biology, or the social sciences require adaptation to the relevant data and validation conditions. Transfer assignments must identify the links between source materials, research judgments, and experimental tests, and explain how new evidence affects subsequent research stages.

Teaching and learning activities

Preparation

Before the first class, students must complete the environment preparation in Getting started. Subsequent sessions specify any additional installations, materials, dependencies, account access, and functionality checks. Lengthy installations and downloads should begin at least a day in advance. Complex configuration and research decisions are addressed in class.

Preparation records must identify system and software versions, material locations, completed checks, and unresolved issues. Students should report environment problems to the instructor before class and use the supplied backup materials where necessary. Download and sign-in issues are resolved outside the taught session.

Class sessions

Each class lasts 60 minutes and combines preparation checks, conceptual instruction, practical analysis, and verification. Exercises use materials obtained in advance and short runs whose outputs can be checked promptly. The final five minutes consolidate common errors, practical outputs, and follow-up tasks.

Across the course, classes address configuration conflicts, evidence comparability, discriminating experiments, state recovery, and identification of learning effects. Full training runs, bulk literature retrieval, and long-term scheduling are completed outside class; their plans, execution requirements, and completion criteria are established during class.

Review and extension

Independent study begins with approximately 15 minutes of unaided recall, followed by verification against the class records. Students then spend 15–20 minutes analyzing sound and flawed examples, recording their judgments before consulting the explanations. Extension assignments develop the same research question further and apply course methods to students’ own research materials.

Review and extension work produce separate records. Extension assignments may take the form of derivations, experiments, or system configurations and must document the process and results. Each session offers several optional paths that can be completed over multiple study periods according to research needs.

Session content and progression

Session Preparation Class activities, 60 minutes Extension following review
1 Writing and figures 30–90 minutes: install compilation and plotting tools, download the exemplar, and rebuild the original draft Revise the paper around its central finding, verify figures, conduct a review, and develop a Skill Paper revision, numerical reconstruction, Skill transfer, or citations and submission
2 Research knowledge bases 20–45 minutes: complete browser and knowledge-base sign-in and obtain three full texts Verify source identities, distribute reading tasks, and turn a disagreement into a discriminating question Version differences, propagation of counterevidence, or rescreening calibration
3 Experimental analysis 20–40 minutes: check Git, the locked environment, and preview materials Freeze predictions, modify experimental code, and conduct short runs, paired analysis, and review Reproducing versioned runs, recovering remote jobs, or deriving intervals and mechanisms
4 Research automation 15–30 minutes: organize earlier outputs and prepare command entry points and a local page Simulate failure, conduct one scheduled review and handover, and design an evaluation of playbook entries Scheduling handover, three-group evaluation of playbook entries, or results-page generation
5 Safety controls 20–40 minutes: download components and the scanner and back up configuration Assign permissions by role, test allowed and denied actions, and check Hook coverage Retesting through other entry points and executors, and verifying the delivery entry point
6 Orchestration theory 10–20 minutes: check the standard library and materials and prepare a candidate playbook entry Define the fixed agent and learning object, decompose effects across three groups, and formulate testable predictions Policy and credit assignment, effect identification, transfer, and cost analysis

Sessions 1–4 are required and taken in sequence. Sessions 5 and 6 are independent electives, each following Session 4; Session 5 is not a prerequisite for Session 6. Each session page specifies preparation checks, the class schedule, comparison examples, and extension materials.

Assessment

For each session taken, review, independent work and transfer assessment are required. Any specified core readings within extension pages are also required; the remaining extension work is optional.

Work assessed Criteria Transfer and extension
Revised paper The central finding and argument are clear; numerical results are traceable to source data Revise a paper using different research materials and respond to an independent review
Research knowledge base Claims have identifiable sources, versions, and page references Add counterevidence and update affected judgments and research questions
Experimental record Results correspond to recorded predictions and identifiable execution versions Add an effective control that distinguishes competing explanations
Automated research workflow Recovery is correct and repeated requests do not duplicate effects Add limited scheduling and retain records of failures and stops
Safety tests Authorized actions succeed and specified unauthorized actions are blocked Retest the same permission boundary through different entry points
Learning evaluation Three-group comparisons distinguish the total effect from the effect of playbook content Evaluate on unseen tasks, report uncertainty intervals, and design a follow-up experiment

The required component concludes with a complete research record and a demonstration of recovery after interruption. Assessment emphasizes evidence traceability, experimental design, interpretation, and correct recovery behavior. The electives require safety test records or a learning evaluation report and protocol, respectively. Execution status and scientific conclusions are assessed separately, with research judgments supported by the relevant evidence. Each session page states its learning outcomes and ends with an assessment section describing how they are judged at three levels: practice on the shared case in class, independent completion after class, and transfer to different material.

Prerequisites and technical requirements

Students should be able to read research literature and be familiar with the data analysis methods used in their field. Prior experience developing agents is not required. A Windows, macOS, or Linux computer is needed, with installation and initial file operations completed as specified in Getting started. Writing and review normally use agents from different vendors. Where only one vendor is available, exercises may use separate new sessions, with the executor identified in the record.

Session 2 requires the class’s open-access full texts in advance; institutional sign-in should be completed beforehand for any subscription material. Session 3 can be completed locally, with GitHub and servers used where available. Session 4 does not require live notifications or deployment. Purchasing API credits or additional cloud servers is not required. Students complete system authorization and account sign-in themselves.

Course references

The course structure draws on the research projects in UChicago AI Agents for Science and the Carpentries’ episode design. The advanced material draws on Berkeley LLM Agents, the Hugging Face Agents Course and DeepLearning.AI Evaluating AI Agents. Agentic Coding in MIT’s Missing Semester shows how the tools enter real work. For tool operation, see Anthropic’s official course and the OpenAI Codex CLI tutorial.
