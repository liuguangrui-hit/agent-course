课程大纲

课程主线

课程沿用带噪标签学习的小实验。第 1 课把已有研究写清楚，第 2 课发现尚未解决的问题，第 3 课做判别实验，第 4 课把这个过程接成可恢复的循环。材料选得小，是为了让你在课堂上看完数据，也看完 Agent 怎样犯错。

同样的做法可以用于材料筛选、计算生物学和社会科学。换成自己的方向时，保留任务之间的关系：哪份原件支持哪条判断，哪次实验能改变这条判断，改变以后谁要接着处理。

每堂课的三个阶段

课前准备

首次上课前先走完第 0 课。以后每课按自己的预备卡安装新增工具并下载材料与依赖，完成登录后运行一次基本可用性检查。安装耗时长的提前一天开始，实际时间取决于网络与设备。课前只需要确认东西能用，复杂配置与学术判断留到课堂。

每课带一份 preflight.md：系统与版本，实际文件位置，已通过的检查，未解决的错误。开课前把卡住的环节告诉老师，按本课备用材料继续学习。下载和登录问题另行补齐，不占用全班的一小时。

课堂学习

每堂课固定 60 分钟。第一段先看准备记录与成品，随后讲清本课的关键判断。中间几段操作只用已经取得的材料和短运行，结果一出现就核查。最后五分钟检查易错点，把成果与下一步写进文件。

这一小时会共同处理配置合并，判断证据是否可比，设计判别实验，练习状态恢复，并分析怎样识别学习效应。完整训练、整批文献获取和长期调度放到课后执行；课堂会先讲清它们的计划与完成判据。

课后复习与深入练习

先花 15 分钟关掉 Agent 复述课堂，再打开文件核查。接着用 15 至 20 分钟判断一组正反例，写出理由后再看对照思路。最后沿课堂同一个问题选一条深入路径，把新方法用到自己的材料上。

复习和拔高分别有产物。复盘写入 review-after-class.md，深入练习留下推导、实验或配置的实际记录。深入页是选做菜单，可分次完成；一次课后不用走完所有路径。

结课作品是自己的一个小工作台。交付一轮完整研究记录，再演示一次中断恢复。自动完成的步数只反映运行情况，研究问题有没有得到回答要看证据。

课前共同条件

准备一台 Windows、macOS 或 Linux 电脑，先按第 0 课完成安装与首次文件读写。两家 Agent 用于分开写作与审阅；暂时只有一家时，用独立新会话完成练习，并注明执行者。

第 2 课需要取得课堂的开放全文；涉及订阅材料时提前完成机构登录。第 3 课可全程用本机，GitHub 与服务器按已有条件选用。第 4 课的真实通知和部署不作为开课条件。课程不要求购买 API 额度或新增云服务器，系统授权和账号登录由你本人完成。

课程参考

课程结构参考 UChicago AI Agents for Science 的科研项目实践，以及 Carpentries 的分节设计。进阶内容对照 Berkeley LLM Agents、Hugging Face Agents Course 和 DeepLearning.AI Evaluating AI Agents。MIT Missing Semester 的 Agentic Coding可对照看工具怎样进入真实工作。工具操作参考 Anthropic 官方课程 与 OpenAI Codex CLI 教程。

Syllabus

The research thread

The course follows a small experiment on learning with noisy labels. Lesson 1 writes up existing research clearly, Lesson 2 finds an open question, Lesson 3 designs and runs an experiment to distinguish explanations, and Lesson 4 connects the process into a loop that can recover. The materials are small so that in class you can see all the data, and also see exactly how the agent makes mistakes.

The same approach works for materials screening, computational biology and the social sciences. When you switch to your own field, keep the relations between tasks: which source supports which judgment, which experiment could change that judgment, and who has to act once it changes.

The three stages of every lesson

Before class: prepare the tools and materials

Before the first class, work through Lesson 0. For each later lesson, follow its preparation card to install the new tools, download materials and dependencies, log in, and run a basic functionality check. Start long installs a day early; the actual time depends on your network and machine. Before class you only need to confirm that things work. Complex configuration and academic judgment are left for class.

Bring a preflight.md to every lesson: system and versions, file locations, checks that passed, unresolved errors. Tell the instructor where you got stuck before class starts, and continue with that lesson’s backup materials. Download and login problems are fixed separately, outside the shared class hour.

In class: work through the key decisions

Every class lasts exactly 60 minutes. The first block looks at preparation records and completed examples, then explains the lesson’s central decision. The middle blocks use only materials already obtained and short runs, and results are checked as soon as they appear. The last five minutes check common mistakes and save the outputs and next steps in files.

In the hour we work through how to merge configuration, compare evidence, design an experiment that separates explanations, recover state, and identify a learning effect. Full training, bulk literature retrieval and long-term scheduling run after class; in class we first make their plans and completion criteria clear.

After class: recall, compare and extend

First spend 15 minutes without the agent, recalling what you learned, then open the files and check. Next spend 15 to 20 minutes comparing examples of sound and flawed approaches, writing your reasons before reading the comparison notes. Finally choose one path that goes deeper into the same question as the class, and apply the new method to your own materials.

Review and extension each produce a record. The review goes into review-after-class.md; the deeper exercise leaves an actual record of a derivation, experiment or configuration. The further-study pages offer optional paths and can be done over several sessions; you need not finish every path after one class.

How the six lessons build

Lesson Before class 60 minutes in class Extension after the review
1 Writing and figures 30 to 90 minutes: install compilation and plotting tools, download the exemplar, rebuild the original draft Restructure the draft around its central finding, check figures, review the draft and create a Skill Revising the narrative or regenerating numbers, testing a Skill on new material or preparing citations and submission files
2 Research knowledge bases 20 to 45 minutes: log in to the browser and knowledge base, download three full texts Check the identity of originals, split the reading of three papers, turn a disagreement into a testable question Version differences, propagation of a counterexample, or rescreening calibration
3 Experimental analysis 20 to 40 minutes: Git and a locked environment working, previews readable Freeze predictions, change code, short runs, paired comparison and review Reproducing a versioned run, recovering remote jobs, or working through intervals and mechanisms
4 Research automation 15 to 30 minutes: gather earlier lessons’ products, prepare a command entry point and local page Simulate a failure, one scheduled review and handover, design an experience test Scheduling takeover, three-group test of experience, or a public view of the results
5 Safety controls 20 to 40 minutes: download components and scanner, back up configuration Assign permissions by role, test allowed and denied actions, check Hook coverage Retest with another entry point and executor, verify the delivery entry point
6 Orchestration theory 10 to 20 minutes: standard library and materials readable, bring one candidate playbook entry Define the origin and what can be learned, decompose effects using three groups, design a testable prediction Policy and credit assignment, effect identification and transfer, cost

Take the four required lessons in order. Lessons 5 and 6 are electives that each follow Lesson 4 independently; Lesson 6 does not require Lesson 5. Each lesson’s own page gives the preparation check, minutes for every block, examples to compare and links to further study.

How to tell you have learned it

Product Check in class After class, with your own materials
Restructured draft A reader can restate one finding, and the numbers trace back to data Switch exemplar and materials, restructure the full paper, and respond to an independent review
Knowledge base Pick one conclusion and find its version and page Add a counterexample paper and update the affected conclusions and questions
Experiment Results are linked to the predictions recorded in advance and to the code and data used Add a control that separates competing explanations
Research loop Recover after an interruption with no duplicated effect Add a small schedule and retain records of failures and stops
Safety boundary Allowed actions succeed; preset out-of-bounds actions are blocked Retest the same boundary through another entry point
Learning plan Three-group comparisons separate the total gain from the content difference Measure on unseen tasks, give an interval and the next experiment

The final project is a small workbench of your own. Hand in the record of one complete round of research, then demonstrate one recovery from interruption. The number of automated steps describes execution; whether the research question was answered depends on the evidence.

Shared requirements before class

Prepare a Windows, macOS or Linux computer and complete installation and the first file read and write following Lesson 0. Agents from two vendors are used to separate writing from review; if you have only one for now, do the exercises with a separate new session and note who did what.

Lesson 2 needs the class’s open-access full texts; if subscription material is involved, finish institutional login in advance. Lesson 3 can run entirely on your own machine; use GitHub and servers if you already have them. Real notifications and deployment are not prerequisites for Lesson 4. The course does not require buying API credit or renting additional cloud servers, and you complete system authorization and account logins yourself.

Course references

The course structure draws on the research projects in UChicago AI Agents for Science and the Carpentries’ episode design. The advanced material draws on Berkeley LLM Agents, the Hugging Face Agents Course and DeepLearning.AI Evaluating AI Agents. Agentic Coding in MIT’s Missing Semester shows how the tools enter real work. For tool operation, see Anthropic’s official course and the OpenAI Codex CLI tutorial.
