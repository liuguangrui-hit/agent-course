Agent 科研

课程简介

本课程围绕人工智能 Agent 在科研中的应用方法，涵盖论文写作与绘图、科研知识库、实验分析、流程自动化、安全控制及编排理论。课程结合专题讲授、案例分析与研究实践，重点培养证据核查、实验设计、可复现研究和 Agent 系统评估能力。

课程目标

完成必修部分后，学生应能够：

  1. 运用 Agent 完成论文表达、数据核查与结果绘图，核验文本、图表和原始数据的一致性。
  2. 构建可追溯的科研知识库，辨析文献结论及其证据，提出可检验的研究问题。
  3. 设计区分竞争解释的对照实验，事先确定分析方案，开展结果分析与复现核查。
  4. 构建具备状态记录和故障恢复能力的自动化科研流程，检验流程衔接与执行结果。

选修部分进一步训练权限边界设计与安全测试，以及固定 Agent 之外的经验学习建模、学习收益评估和迁移分析。

适用人群与先修要求

课程面向拟将 Agent 应用于科研工作的硕士研究生和博士研究生，也可供研究生导师及科研人员参考。学习者应具备基本的科研文献阅读能力，了解所在领域常用的数据分析方法。课程不要求已有 Agent 开发经验；统计推断与形式化分析的进阶内容可结合课后材料学习。

上课前须按课前准备完成软件安装、账号登录及环境检查。各课所需材料和检查要求见相应课程页面。

课程内容

课程设四次必修课和两次选修课，每次课堂教学 60 分钟。第 1 至第 4 课按顺序完成；第 5、6 课在必修部分之后选修。第 0 单元为课前准备，不计入六次课堂教学。

学习评价

各所修单元的复习、独立实践与迁移评价为必做要求；深入专题中指定的必读片段须完成，其余内容按需选做。

学习评价以实践成果与方法论证为依据，重点考察来源可追溯性、数据与图表一致性、实验设计、结果解释及流程恢复能力。评价分三个层次：课堂训练中的方法运用，课后独立完成的练习，以及更换材料或条件后的迁移检验。各课的学习目标对应该课及其课后练习，并非全部在 60 分钟课堂内独立达成。必修部分须完成一轮可复查的研究记录，并演示中断恢复；选修部分提交相应的安全测试记录或学习收益评估记录与方案。具体要求见课程大纲。

AI Agents for Research

Course overview

This graduate course examines the use of AI agents in scientific research. Topics include academic writing and figures, research knowledge bases, experimental analysis, research automation, safety controls, and orchestration theory. Lectures, case studies, and practical assignments develop students’ ability to assess evidence, design experiments, conduct reproducible research, and evaluate agent systems.

Learning outcomes

On completion of the required sessions, students should be able to:

  1. Organize a research paper around its central finding, verify that its numbers and figures agree with the underlying data, and use independent review to improve it.
  2. Verify literature sources and their versions, distinguish observations from synthesis and hypothesis, and turn disagreements in the literature into testable research questions.
  3. Design controlled experiments that distinguish competing explanations, specify analysis plans in advance, interpret paired results with their uncertainty, and maintain reproducible records.
  4. Represent research workflows as explicit states with recovery procedures, and evaluate whether recorded experience improves performance on unseen tasks.

The electives extend these outcomes to the design and testing of permission boundaries, and to the formal modeling and evaluation of learning from external experience while the underlying agent remains fixed, including transfer across agents. Each session page states its own learning outcomes; reproducing the course case study is the means of practice, not the outcome itself.

Intended audience and prerequisites

The course is intended for master’s and doctoral students who plan to use agents in research. It may also be useful to research supervisors and other researchers. Students should be able to read research literature and be familiar with the data analysis methods used in their field. Prior experience developing agents is not required. Advanced material on statistical inference and formal analysis is supported by further-study readings and exercises.

Before attending, students must complete software installation, account sign-in, and environment checks in Getting started. Each session specifies its materials and preparation requirements.

Course structure

There are six 60-minute sessions: four required sessions followed by two electives. Sessions 1–4 are taken in sequence. Unit 0 covers preparation and is separate from the six taught sessions.

Teaching format

Stage Activities Outputs
Preparation Software installation, dependency and material downloads, account access, and functionality checks A preparation record and accessible local materials
Class Lectures, complex configuration, evidence assessment, short practical exercises, and verification Practical outputs with recorded versions and sources
Review and extension Conceptual review, analysis of sound and flawed examples, and related extension work A review record and an extension assignment

A shared case study on learning with noisy labels connects writing, literature analysis, experimentation, and workflow management. Class time is devoted to research decisions and complex operations. Full training runs, bulk literature retrieval, and long-running tasks are completed outside class. The Toolbox lists tools and environment requirements by stage.

Assessment

For each session taken, review, independent work and transfer assessment are required. Any specified core readings within extension pages are also required; the remaining extension work is optional.

Assessment is based on practical work and methodological justification, at three levels: practice on the shared case in class, independent completion after class, and transfer of the same methods to different material. Criteria include source traceability, consistency between data and figures, experimental design, interpretation of results, and workflow recovery. The required component concludes with an auditable record of one research cycle and a demonstration of recovery after interruption. Elective work comprises safety test records or a learning evaluation report and protocol, as appropriate. Details are given in the Syllabus.
